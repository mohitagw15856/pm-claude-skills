# Coaching Season Plan

Without a season plan, training becomes a reaction to last weekend's result. This skill sets out what the team is trying to become by the end of the season, divides the weeks into phases with a clear emphasis, and keeps load sensible around fixtures. It is written for volunteer and part-time coaches as well as full-time ones, and it adapts to the age and level of the players.

## What This Skill Produces

- Two to four season aims, at least one about development and one about enjoyment or retention for youth teams
- A phase table: dates, emphasis, what success looks like
- A week-by-week outline with session themes and fixtures
- A load calendar that marks heavy, moderate and light weeks
- Player development priorities by group or position
- Review points with the questions to ask at each

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:

- Sport, age group, level, squad size and typical attendance
- Season dates, fixtures or competition format, holidays and exam periods
- Sessions per week, length, facilities and equipment
- Where the team is now: strengths, gaps, last season's lessons
- The club's or school's philosophy and any governing-body guidance for the age group
- Coaching help available, and any players with known injuries or needs

## Framework

1. **Aims before content.** For children, development, playing time and enjoyment come before results. Say so in the aims.
2. **Phases.** Pre-season (build fitness and basics), early season (settle roles and principles), mid-season (refine, manage load), run-in (sharpen, keep fresh), off-season (rest and review).
3. **One emphasis per block.** Each two to four week block has a main theme and a secondary one. Everything else is maintained.
4. **Load follows fixtures.** Hardest session early in the week, light work the day before a match, a lighter week after a run of fixtures. For young players, cap volume and vary the activity.
5. **Development priorities per group.** Two or three per position group or ability band, written as things a player will be able to do.
6. **Fair opportunity.** State the playing-time and rotation approach, especially for youth.
7. **Review.** After each phase: what improved, what did not, what changes.

## Output Format

**Aims**

**Phases**

|---|---|---|---|---|

**Week by week**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Development priorities**
| Group | Priority | A player can... |
|---|---|---|

**Playing time and rotation:** [approach]

**Review points**
| When | Questions | Who is involved |
|---|---|---|

**Assumptions and gaps**
- [What was assumed about fixtures, attendance or level]
- [Information that would most change the plan]

## Quality Checks

- [ ] Aims match the age and level, and youth plans put development first
- [ ] Each block has one main emphasis
- [ ] Light sessions sit before matches and lighter weeks follow busy runs
- [ ] Holidays and exam periods are marked
- [ ] Development priorities are written as observable abilities
- [ ] Review points are dated

## Anti-Patterns

- **Do not copy an adult programme for children.**
- **Do not plan every week as a heavy week.**
- **Do not change the theme every session** in response to one result.
- **Do not give medical or rehabilitation advice.** Injured players follow a qualified professional's guidance.
- **Do not leave playing time unstated** for youth teams.

## Example Trigger Phrases

- "Plan a season for my under-11 football team, two sessions a week."
- "Build a six-week pre-season for a club netball side."
- "What should our themes be each month for a school basketball team?"
- "We have exams in May. Rework the plan around that."
