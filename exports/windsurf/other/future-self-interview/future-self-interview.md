# Future Self Interview Skill

Present-you is a bad advisor on big decisions — flooded with immediate emotion,
sunk cost, and the loudest current pressure. Future-you, looking back, tends to see
what actually mattered: which fears were noise, which small brave thing changed
everything, what you'd have regretted. This skill runs a structured interview with
that older self — not as mysticism or prediction, but as a disciplined perspective
shift that surfaces the values and patterns present-you can't feel through the fog.
It's the "regret from the deathbed" and "advice to your younger self" instincts,
made into a repeatable, grounded conversation.

## What This Skill Produces

- A **structured interview**: present-you asks, future-you (5 or 10 years on, or at a
  chosen life stage) answers — built from the user's *own* stated values, history,
  and patterns, not generic wisdom
- The **themes it surfaces**: what future-you keeps returning to, the fears they
  dismiss, the thing they wish present-you had done sooner
- A **reality check**: separating genuine long-view insight from wishful projection —
  future-you is honest, not a hype-man
- **One concrete next step** the interview points toward — because perspective
  without an action is just a nice feeling

## Required Inputs

Ask for (if not already provided):
- The decision or stuck point, and what's making it hard right now
- The user's actual values and what they've regretted or been proud of before (the
  raw material — future-you is built from these, not invented)
- The time horizon that fits (5 years for a career/relationship fork, the deathbed
  view for a life-shape question)
- What present-you is most afraid of, and most tempted by

## Framework

1. **Build future-you from the user, not from platitudes.** The older self speaks in
   the user's own values and history — if the user has always regretted playing it
   safe, future-you knows that; if they've been burned by impulsiveness, future-you
   remembers. Generic "follow your heart" future-selves are useless; a future-you
   made of the user's real patterns is uncannily useful.
2. **Interview, don't monologue.** Present-you asks real questions — "was it worth
   it?", "what did I waste time worrying about?", "what do you wish I'd done this
   year?", "what mattered that I can't see yet?" — and future-you answers specifically.
   The Q&A structure does the perspective work; a lecture doesn't.
3. **Let future-you be honest, not reassuring.** The point isn't comfort — it's the
   longer view, which sometimes says "yes you should make the scary change" and
   sometimes "the thing you're agonizing over won't matter; the thing you're ignoring
   will." Future-you can deliver an uncomfortable truth; a purely soothing one has
   failed the exercise.
4. **Separate insight from wishful projection.** Flag where the "future self" is really
   present-you's wishful thinking wearing a costume vs a genuine values-consistent
   long view. The honest version notes: "this answer might be your fear/hope talking —
   here's how to tell."
5. **Land on one step.** Perspective that changes nothing is entertainment. The
   interview ends by asking future-you: "what's the one thing you wish present-me
   would do this week?" — small, concrete, doable now.

## Output Format

```
## The setup
[The decision · the horizon · who future-you is, built from your values/patterns]

## The interview
Present-you: [real question]
Future-you: [specific, honest, in-your-own-values answer]
[…several exchanges — worth it? / what I wasted worry on / what mattered / the regret]

## What future-you kept returning to
[The themes · the fears they dismissed · the thing to do sooner]

## Insight vs wishful thinking
[Where this is genuine long-view · where it might be hope/fear in disguise, and how
to tell]

## The one thing to do this week
[Small, concrete, now]
```

## Quality Checks

- [ ] Future-you is built from the user's actual values/history, not generic wisdom
- [ ] It's an interview (Q&A), not a monologue, and the questions are real ones
- [ ] Future-you says at least one uncomfortable/non-reassuring thing where honest
- [ ] Wishful-projection is flagged and distinguished from genuine long view
- [ ] It ends on one concrete, this-week step

## Anti-Patterns

- [ ] Do not present this as prediction, fortune-telling, or mysticism — it's a
      perspective-shift tool; future-you is a lens, not an oracle
- [ ] Do not make future-you a pure hype-man — reassurance-only defeats the long view
- [ ] Do not import generic life-advice — a future self that could belong to anyone
      helps no one
- [ ] Do not let it end without an action, or it's just a pleasant daydream
- [ ] Do not use it to rationalize a decision already made — if the user's steering
      future-you to a predetermined answer, name that gently
