# Property Tax Appeal

Property tax is based on an assessed value that's often wrong — and a successful appeal can cut your bill for years. Most people never challenge it because they don't know how. This helps you check whether you're genuinely over-assessed, assemble the comparables and evidence that win appeals, and file before the (strict) deadline — while being clear the process is local and this isn't tax or legal advice.

## What This Skill Produces

- **An over-assessment check** — comparing your assessed value to recent sales of similar properties and your home's real condition
- **The evidence pack** — comparable sales, photos of defects/issues, an independent valuation if warranted, and assessment errors (wrong size/features)
- **The appeal steps & deadline** — how to file with your assessor/board and the firm deadline (often a short annual window)
- **A savings estimate** — roughly what a successful reduction is worth per year
- **Hearing expectations** — what the informal review or board hearing involves and how to present
- **A locality flag** — process, deadlines, and grounds vary by jurisdiction; not tax/legal advice

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **Your assessment** — the assessed value, the tax bill, and the assessment date
- **Your property** — size, features, condition, recent purchase price if any
- **Comparables** — recent sales of similar nearby homes (or a request to help find them)
- **Any errors** — wrong square footage, bedroom count, lot size, or condition on record
- **Location** — determines the process, deadline, and appeal body

## Framework: Prove It's Too High, File On Time

1. **Test the assessment.** Compare your assessed value to recent sales of genuinely similar properties and to your home's actual condition — an appeal needs it to be demonstrably too high, not just "high."
2. **Check the record for errors.** Assessors work from data that's often wrong (overstated size, features, or condition) — a factual error is one of the easiest wins.
3. **Build comparable-sales evidence.** The core of most appeals is comps: similar homes that sold for less than your assessment implies. Assemble the strongest few.
4. **File before the deadline.** Appeal windows are short and strict; establish the deadline first and file with the correct body.
5. **Present cleanly at the hearing.** Lead with the comps and the number you believe is correct; be factual, not emotional.

## Output Format

### Property tax appeal: assessed [value] · [location]

**Over-assessed?** vs comparables/condition → [likely yes / borderline / probably not].
**Evidence to build:** [comps · record errors · photos · valuation if needed].
**File:** with [assessor/board], by [deadline] — [informal review vs formal appeal].
**Worth:** ~[annual saving if reduced].
**Hearing:** lead with comps + your supported value; factual and brief.

> Process, deadlines, and grounds vary by jurisdiction. Not tax or legal advice — confirm with your local assessor's office, and consider a professional for high-value properties.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Tests the assessment against real comparables/condition
- [ ] Checks the assessment record for factual errors
- [ ] Builds a comparable-sales evidence pack
- [ ] Emphasizes the strict filing deadline
- [ ] Estimates the annual savings
- [ ] Flags that process is local / not tax-legal advice

## Anti-Patterns
- **Appealing on "it feels high"** with no comparables.
- **Missing the short filing window.**
- **Ignoring easy record-error wins.**
- **Emotional hearing pitches** instead of comps and numbers.
- **Asserting one process** across all jurisdictions.
