# Stop Overthinking This

Some decisions deserve careful thought. Most of the ones you're spinning on don't — they're small, reversible, or the options are so close that the deliberation costs more than any wrong choice would. This calls that out, forces a pick with a simple rule, and gives you permission to move on. The goal isn't the perfect choice; it's to stop paying rent on a decision that doesn't matter.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A does-this-deserve-it check** — is this actually a big/irreversible decision, or are you overthinking a small one?
- **The "it's close" reveal** — the recognition that if you've deliberated this long, the options are probably near-equal, which means it barely matters
- **A forced pick** — a simple mechanism to just choose (a rule, a gut-call, a coin flip you react to)
- **The real cost named** — what the overthinking itself is costing (time, energy, the other things not getting done)
- **Permission to move on** — an explicit close, so the loop actually ends

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The decision** — what you're spinning on
- **How long you've deliberated** — a clue to how close the options are
- **Reversibility & stakes** — can you change it later; how much rides on it
- **What you keep going back and forth between** — the options

## Framework: Right-Size It, Then Force The Call

1. **Check if it deserves deliberation.** Big and irreversible? Take your time. Small or reversible? The overthinking is the problem, not the choice.
2. **Use the deliberation as a signal.** If you've been stuck this long, the options are probably close to equal — which is proof the choice doesn't matter much.
3. **Name the cost of spinning.** The time, energy, and mental space the loop is eating — usually far more than the difference between the options.
4. **Force the pick.** A simple rule ("pick the one you leaned toward first"), a gut call, or a coin flip you notice your reaction to — anything that ends it.
5. **Close the loop.** Explicit permission to decide and move on, and to not revisit it — the decision is now made.

## Output Format

### Overthinking: [the decision] · deliberated for [time]

**Does this deserve it?** [no — small/reversible → stop / yes — genuinely big → this isn't your skill].
**The tell:** you've gone back and forth this long → the options are close → it barely matters.
**What the spinning is costing:** [time / energy / everything else].
**👉 Pick:** [forced-choice mechanism] → go with that.
**Now move on.** It's decided. Don't reopen it.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Checks whether the decision actually deserves deliberation
- [ ] Reframes long deliberation as proof the options are close
- [ ] Names the real cost of the overthinking
- [ ] Forces an actual pick with a simple mechanism
- [ ] Gives explicit permission to move on
- [ ] Redirects genuinely big decisions to real deliberation

## Anti-Patterns
- **Adding more analysis** to an overthinking problem.
- **Forcing a snap call** on a genuinely big, irreversible decision.
- **No actual pick** at the end.
- **Leaving the loop open** to be reopened later.

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "I'm massively overthinking which laptop to buy — just help me decide."
- "I keep going back and forth on this. Make me choose."
- "This shouldn't be this hard. Help me stop spinning."
- "I've spent an hour on a tiny decision. End it."
- "Help me just pick and move on."
