# Tenant Rights Explainer

Renters often don't act because they don't know whether they *can* — is that rent increase legal, does the landlord have to fix the heating, is this eviction notice valid? This explains the rights likely in play for your situation in plain language, gives the practical next move (usually: put it in writing and keep records), and points you to the authoritative local source — because tenancy law varies enormously by place, and this isn't legal advice.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A plain-English rights read** — the protections likely relevant (habitability/repairs, notice periods, deposit rules, rent-increase limits, entry rules, eviction process)
- **Practical next steps** — usually a written request/complaint, on the record, with a reasonable deadline
- **Evidence to keep** — photos, dates, messages, the tenancy agreement, payment records
- **Where to get authoritative help** — local tenant unions, housing authorities, or legal aid for your area
- **A strong locality flag** — tenancy law is highly local; everything must be checked against your jurisdiction and it isn't legal advice

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The situation** — repairs, rent increase, eviction/notice, deposit, entry, harassment, or other
- **The specifics** — what happened, when, what was said/written
- **Your tenancy** — type of agreement, how long, what it says (if you have it)
- **Location** — the key input; rights vary by country/state/city
- **What you want** — the repair done, the increase challenged, to stay, your deposit back

## Framework: Name The Right, Act In Writing, Verify Locally

1. **Identify the likely right.** Map the situation to the general protection at play (repairs/habitability, valid-notice requirements, deposit-return rules, rent-increase limits, quiet enjoyment) — as a starting point, not a ruling.
2. **Act in writing.** The strongest practical move is almost always a clear, dated written request/complaint with a reasonable deadline — it asserts the right and creates a record.
3. **Preserve evidence.** Photos, the agreement, messages, payment history, and notices are what make a claim stick.
4. **Point to authority.** Direct the person to the local tenant union, housing/authority body, or legal aid that can give binding, jurisdiction-specific help.
5. **Flag the locality hard.** Notice periods, caps, and processes differ by place and change — present everything as "likely / check locally," never as a definitive legal answer.

## Output Format

### Tenant situation: [issue] · location: [x]

**The right likely at play:** [plain-English explanation] — *general; confirm for your area.*
**Do this:** [written request/complaint + reasonable deadline] — template line: "[…]".
**Keep:** [tenancy agreement · photos · dated messages · payment records · notices].
**Get authoritative help:** [local tenant union / housing authority / legal aid].

> Tenancy law is highly local and this isn't legal advice. Verify with your local housing authority or a tenant-rights service before acting on anything with legal weight.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Identifies the general right relevant to the situation
- [ ] Recommends acting in writing with a record
- [ ] Lists the evidence to preserve
- [ ] Points to authoritative local resources
- [ ] Flags strongly that tenancy law is local / not legal advice
- [ ] Tailors next steps to what the tenant wants

## Anti-Patterns
- **Stating rights as definitive** across jurisdictions.
- **Advising verbal-only** action with no written record.
- **No evidence guidance** — weakening any future claim.
- **Skipping the local authority/legal-aid pointer.**
- **Encouraging risky self-help** (e.g. withholding rent) without a locality/legal caveat.

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "My landlord won't fix the broken heating — what are my rights?"
- "Is this rent increase even legal?"
- "I got an eviction notice — is it valid and what do I do?"
- "My landlord keeps entering without notice. Can they do that?"
- "How do I get my deposit back when my landlord won't return it?"
