# Car-Buying Negotiation

Dealerships negotiate cars for a living; most buyers do it every few years — that asymmetry is the whole game. This levels it: research the real target price, negotiate the out-the-door number (not the monthly payment), keep the trade-in and financing as separate battles, and decline the high-margin add-ons — with the confidence that you'll walk if the deal isn't right.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A target-price approach** — how to research a fair price and negotiate the total out-the-door cost, not the monthly payment
- **Tactics & counters** — the common dealership moves (monthly-payment focus, four-square, "let me ask my manager," urgency, lowball trade) and how to handle each
- **Separate the battles** — negotiating price, trade-in value, and financing independently so they can't be shuffled
- **Add-ons to decline** — the high-margin extras (paint protection, extended warranties, fabric guard, etc.) and which are worth it
- **Financing guidance** — get pre-approved so the dealer's rate must compete, and watch the loan term/total cost
- **A walk-away plan** — the number and the readiness to leave, which is your real leverage

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The car** — make/model/trim, new or used, and any specific one you're eyeing
- **Your research** — do you know the fair/market price, or need the approach
- **Trade-in** — are you trading a vehicle
- **Financing** — cash, dealer finance, or pre-approved elsewhere
- **Your position** — how urgently you need it, and your walk-away willingness

## Framework: Out-The-Door Price, Battles Separated, Ready To Walk

1. **Negotiate the total, not the monthly.** Dealers steer to monthly payments to hide the real price and stretch the term — anchor on the out-the-door number.
2. **Know your target first.** Research market/fair pricing so you negotiate from a number, not vibes; a slow-moving or ideal deal informs your target.
3. **Keep the three deals separate.** Settle the car price before discussing trade-in, and arrange financing independently — bundling is how margin hides.
4. **Get pre-approved financing.** A pre-approval forces the dealer's rate to beat it; without it, they profit on the loan.
5. **Decline the add-ons.** Most F&I extras are high-margin and optional — decline politely; evaluate the few (like a warranty) on their own merits.
6. **Be ready to walk.** A firm walk-away number and genuine willingness to leave is the strongest leverage you have — and verify current prices/rates, which move.

## Output Format

**Target:** research [fair/market out-the-door price] — negotiate the *total*, not the monthly.
**Expect & counter**
- Monthly-payment focus → "let's talk out-the-door total."
- "Ask my manager" / urgency → [hold; you're not in a hurry].
- Lowball trade → [get it valued separately first].
**Keep separate:** car price → then trade-in → then financing.
**Get pre-approved** financing so their rate must beat it.
**Decline:** [paint/fabric protection, gap unless worth it, etc.]; consider [warranty] on merits.
**Walk-away:** your number is [x] — and mean it.

> Verify current market pricing, incentives, and finance rates before you go — they change constantly.

## Anti-Patterns
- **Negotiating the monthly payment** — the dealer's favorite trap.
- **No target price** — negotiating blind.
- **Bundling trade-in and financing** into the price.
- **Taking dealer financing** without a pre-approval to beat.
- **Accepting add-ons** by default.
- **No willingness to walk** — forfeiting all leverage.
