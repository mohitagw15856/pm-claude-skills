# Blended-Family Plan

Blending families is rarely the instant-harmony montage — it's two sets of routines, loyalties, and expectations meeting at once. It goes best when the adults plan the roles and rules in advance, move at the *kids'* pace, and don't force closeness. This lays out a realistic approach to step-parent roles, house rules, relationship-building, and the ex/loyalty dynamics — honest that it takes time.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A situation read** — the ages, the histories, and the specific sensitivities (recent split, grief, resistance) that shape the plan
- **Step-parent role & discipline** — an approach where the biological parent leads discipline early and the step-parent builds a relationship first
- **Rules & routines alignment** — merging house rules, chores, and routines fairly, and coordinating across two homes where possible
- **Relationship-building at each child's pace** — one-on-one time, low pressure, and not forcing "love" or a "new family" narrative
- **Exes & loyalty binds** — reducing conflict with co-parents and easing kids' loyalty conflicts
- **Realistic expectations** — a timeline measured in years, not weeks; when a family counselor helps

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The families** — kids' ages, who lives where, custody arrangements
- **The stage** — dating, moving in, newly blended, or struggling
- **The histories** — how recent the previous relationships ended, any grief
- **The friction** — what's hard now (a resistant child, discipline clashes, an ex)
- **Your goal** — a smooth start, or fixing a specific tension

## Framework: Slow, Fair, Kid-Paced

1. **Read the sensitivities.** Ages, recency of the split/loss, and each child's resistance drive everything — start from their reality, not the adults' timeline.
2. **Get the step-parent role right.** Early on, the biological parent should lead discipline while the step-parent focuses on building trust and a relationship — stepping into "enforcer" too soon breeds resentment.
3. **Align rules and routines fairly.** Agree consistent house rules and chores that don't favor one set of kids, and coordinate with the other home where you can.
4. **Build relationships at the child's pace.** One-on-one time and shared low-pressure activities; don't force affection or a "we're one big family now" story kids aren't ready for.
5. **Manage exes and loyalty binds.** Keep co-parent conflict away from the kids and reassure them they don't have to choose or replace anyone.
6. **Expect it to take time.** Blending is a multi-year process; name that, and flag a family counselor for serious or stuck dynamics. (This isn't therapy.)

**Build bonds (kid-paced):** [one-on-one · low pressure · no forced "love"].

> Supportive guidance, not therapy or clinical advice — for serious struggles, a family therapist can help.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Reads the specific ages/histories/sensitivities
- [ ] Gets the step-parent discipline/relationship sequence right
- [ ] Aligns rules/routines fairly across both sets of kids
- [ ] Builds relationships at the children's pace, no forcing
- [ ] Addresses exes and kids' loyalty binds
- [ ] Sets realistic multi-year expectations and flags counseling

## Anti-Patterns
- **Instant-family expectations** — forcing closeness fast.
- **Step-parent as immediate disciplinarian** — breeds resentment.
- **Unfair rules** favoring one set of kids.
- **Making kids choose** or feel disloyal.
- **Letting ex-conflict** spill onto the children.

## Example Trigger Phrases

- "My partner's child is resisting me — what do I do?"
- "How do we merge two sets of house rules fairly?"
- "Help our blended family get off to a good start."
