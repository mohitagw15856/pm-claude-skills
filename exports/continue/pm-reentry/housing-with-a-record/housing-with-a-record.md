# Housing With a Record

Background-screening walls keep many people with records from renting even when they can pay. The way through is the same as with jobs: apply where records don't auto-disqualify, present it briefly and honestly with proof you're a reliable tenant, and know the fair-housing limits on blanket bans. This builds that path so a record narrows the search instead of ending it.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A target list** — private/individual landlords (who screen case-by-case), second-chance and nonprofit housing, transitional programs, and how to find them
- **An explanation letter** — a short, honest note you can offer: name it briefly, show what changed, and lead with why you're a dependable tenant
- **A trust packet** — the documents that offset a record: references (employer, prior landlord, case manager), income/benefits proof, rental history, rehabilitation evidence
- **Your fair-housing rights** — how blanket "no record" policies can violate fair-housing guidance, and individualized-assessment expectations
- **A search strategy** — prioritizing individual landlords over big-screener corporate complexes, and being upfront to avoid wasted application fees
- **A resource pointer** — housing counselors, reentry housing programs, legal aid

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The situation** — record type/age, whether sealed/expungeable
- **What you need** — budget, location, household, timeline
- **Your strengths** — income/benefits, references, stable history
- **Where** — region (fair-housing and record rules vary)

## Framework: Apply Smart, Offer Proof, Know the Limits

1. **Aim at individual landlords and second-chance programs.** They assess people, not just a screening flag — far higher hit rate than corporate complexes with auto-deny rules.
2. **Be upfront to save fees.** Ask about their screening before paying an application fee; disclose briefly rather than getting auto-denied after paying.
3. **Lead with reliability.** Income proof, references, and rental history do more than any explanation — assemble the trust packet first.
4. **Offer a short honest note.** Name it, show change, pivot to dependability — same discipline as a job disclosure.
5. **Know the fair-housing limits.** Blanket record bans can violate fair-housing guidance; individualized assessment is often required — cite it calmly if useful, and route to legal aid for denials that smell discriminatory.

## Anti-Patterns
- **Mass-applying** to corporate complexes that auto-deny records.
- **Paying application fees** without asking about screening first.
- **A long defensive letter** instead of proof of reliability.
- **Advising concealment** rather than brief honesty.
- **Ignoring fair-housing rights** and rehabilitation evidence.
