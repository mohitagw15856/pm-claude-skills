# Credit From Scratch Skill

One of the rudest shocks of moving countries: your excellent credit history back home
counts for nothing — credit doesn't cross borders — so a financially responsible adult
is suddenly "unscorable" and gets rejected for a phone contract, an apartment, or a
card. The system reads no-history the same as bad-history until you build a local track
record. This skill is the plan to build that record deliberately and fast: the starter
products that actually *report* to the local bureaus, the behaviors that build score,
the newcomer traps that waste months, and a realistic timeline. It's educational, not
financial advice, and routes score/report specifics to the official bureaus.

## What This Skill Produces

- A **credit-building plan**: the specific first steps that create a reporting history —
  because using cash or a debit card builds nothing; only products that report to the
  bureaus count
- The **starter products that report**: newcomer-friendly options (secured cards,
  credit-builder loans/products, becoming an authorized user, newcomer banking programs)
  and how to tell if a product actually reports
- The **behaviors that build score fast**: on-time payments (the dominant factor), low
  utilization, keeping the first account open, not rate-shopping into hard inquiries
- The **traps and a timeline**: the newcomer mistakes (applying widely and racking up
  rejections, high utilization, closing the builder account) and a realistic "usable
  score in ~6–12 months" arc — routed to the official bureaus to monitor

## Required Inputs

Ask for (if not already provided):
- The country (credit systems differ — US FICO/bureaus, UK CRAs, others) and status
  (some products need residency/SSN-equivalent)
- The goal driving it (renting, a car loan, a mortgage down the line, just a card) and the
  timeframe
- Current banking (a local account? a job/income? any product opened yet?)
- Whether there's anyone (partner, family) already established locally who could add them
  as an authorized user

## Framework

1. **Explain why the reset happened.** Credit history is national; bureaus in the new
   country have no file on you, and "no file" reads as risk. Some banks consider foreign
   history or offer newcomer programs, but generally you're starting the local record from
   zero — knowing this stops the wasted energy of applying for mainstream products that
   will reject a thin file.
2. **Open something that reports — fast.** The core move: get a product that reports to the
   local bureaus. Typically a secured card (you deposit, it reports like a normal card), a
   credit-builder product, a newcomer bank program, or being added as an authorized user
   on an established person's account. Confirm each actually reports (some don't) — that's
   the whole point.
3. **Feed it the score-building behavior.** Payment history dominates: pay on time, every
   time, ideally in full. Keep utilization low (well under the limit). Keep the first
   account open (age of history matters). Avoid a flurry of applications — each rejection
   and hard inquiry sets you back. A little, done consistently, beats a lot done anxiously.
4. **Dodge the newcomer traps.** The classic self-sabotage: applying to many lenders at
   once and collecting rejections; maxing a low starter limit (high utilization tanks
   score); closing the builder account once approved for something better; assuming a debit
   card or paying rent builds credit when it often doesn't (unless via a reporting scheme).
5. **Set a realistic timeline and monitor.** A usable score generally takes ~6–12 months of
   clean history, enough for many rentals and cards; mortgages want longer. Monitor via the
   official bureaus (often free), watch the file build, and graduate to mainstream products
   as the score supports it — routed to the bureaus, since scoring specifics are theirs.

## Quality Checks

- [ ] The "credit doesn't transfer" reset is explained so the user stops chasing
      mainstream products
- [ ] The first move is a product confirmed to *report* to the local bureaus
- [ ] Score-building behaviors lead with on-time payment and low utilization
- [ ] The newcomer traps (rejection-stacking, maxing, closing the builder) are named
- [ ] A realistic timeline and official-bureau monitoring are included, with the
      not-advice line

## Anti-Patterns

- [ ] Do not give specific financial/product advice or recommend a particular lender —
      explain categories and route to official sources
- [ ] Do not assert scoring rules or numbers as fact — they're country- and bureau-
      specific; route to the bureaus
- [ ] Do not suggest applying widely to "see what sticks" — rejection-stacking is the
      classic newcomer mistake
- [ ] Do not imply debit cards or ordinary rent payments build credit — only reporting
      products/schemes do
- [ ] Do not promise a fast mortgage-ready score — set honest timelines by goal
