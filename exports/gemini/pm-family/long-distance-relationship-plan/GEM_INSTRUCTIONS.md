# Long-Distance Relationship Plan

Distance doesn't kill relationships — drift, mismatched expectations, and no end in sight do. The couples who thrive apart are intentional: a communication rhythm that isn't exhausting, shared experiences that create "us" moments, a realistic visit plan, and honest agreement on where it's heading. This builds that plan for your situation.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A communication rhythm** — a sustainable cadence across schedules and time zones (quality over constant), avoiding both drift and burnout
- **Shared experiences** — ways to *do things together* across the distance (watch, play, cook, plan) so it's not just status updates
- **A visit plan** — frequency, cost-sharing, and making visits count (not just catching up on chores)
- **Handling the hard parts** — jealousy, loneliness, resentment, and the "grass is greener" wobble, addressed directly
- **The end-goal conversation** — an honest talk about the plan to close the distance, so you're apart *toward* something

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The situation** — how far, time-zone gap, how long apart, and why (work, study, temporary)
- **Both schedules** — work/life rhythms that shape when you can connect
- **The stage** — new relationship or established going long-distance
- **The pain points** — what's hard now (frequency, jealousy, feeling disconnected)
- **The horizon** — is there a plan/timeline to be in the same place

## Framework: Intentional, Shared, Toward An End

1. **Quality over constant.** Agree a rhythm that fits both lives and time zones — a few real connections beat all-day texting that becomes obligation.
2. **Do things together.** Shared activities (a show, a game, cooking the same meal, planning a trip) create "us" memories that mere check-ins don't.
3. **Make visits real and planned.** Set a realistic visit cadence and budget, share costs fairly, and protect visit time for connection, not just admin.
4. **Name the hard parts.** Loneliness, jealousy, and resentment are normal — build in honesty, reassurance habits, and independent lives so neither is waiting by the phone.
5. **Keep an eye on the horizon.** LDR is far more sustainable with a shared end goal and rough timeline — have that conversation, even if the answer is "we're figuring it out."

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Communication rhythm fits both schedules/time zones and avoids burnout
- [ ] Includes shared *activities*, not just check-ins
- [ ] Has a realistic, fairly-shared visit plan
- [ ] Addresses the emotional hard parts directly
- [ ] Includes the end-goal/timeline conversation
- [ ] Encourages independent lives alongside the relationship

## Anti-Patterns
- **All-day texting** that becomes an exhausting obligation.
- **Only status updates**, no shared experiences.
- **No end goal** — distance with no light at the end drifts.
- **Ignoring jealousy/loneliness** until they fester.
- **One person carrying** all the visits/cost/effort.
