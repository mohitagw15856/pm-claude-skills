# State-Owned Enterprise Interview (国企面试)

State-owned and central enterprises recruit large numbers of graduates and experienced hires, and their processes differ from private technology companies: written tests often include aptitude and knowledge of the enterprise and its sector, interviews weigh stability, teamwork and fit with the organisation, and there is usually a background check before an offer. This skill prepares for each stage and helps compare the offer with private-sector options.

Write in Simplified Chinese unless asked otherwise. Stages and content vary widely by enterprise and region; the recruitment notice (招聘公告) is the source of truth.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A stage map**: application, written test, interviews, physical check, background check and offer, marked to confirm against the notice
- **A written-test plan**: aptitude (行测-style questions), professional knowledge, and the enterprise's business and recent news
- **A question bank** with answer points: self-introduction, motivation, stability, teamwork, professional questions, and scenario questions
- **A mock interview** asked one question at a time, with feedback
- **An offer comparison** against private-sector options on the dimensions that matter to the person

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The enterprise, the post and the recruitment notice**, or a summary of it
- **Background**: degree, major, experience, and any certificates the post asks for
- **Interview format**, if known: structured interview, semi-structured, group discussion, or professional interview
- **Other offers or options** the person is weighing

## Framework

1. **Read the notice closely.** Eligibility (age, degree, major, certificates), location and any service commitment. A mismatch here ends the application regardless of the interview.
2. **Know the enterprise.** Its main business, its place in the sector, recent major projects and the strategy language it uses in public reports. Interviewers expect candidates to know what the enterprise does.
3. **Prepare the written test.** Timed aptitude practice, the professional knowledge the post lists, and current affairs relevant to the sector.
4. **Answer for fit.** Common lines: "为什么选择国企 / 我们公司？", "能否接受外派或基层锻炼？", "你的职业规划是什么？", "和同事意见不合怎么办？", "你在团队中通常是什么角色？". Strong answers are honest, specific and show the person has thought about the role's real conditions.
5. **Ask good questions.** About training, rotation, the first year's work and how performance is assessed.
6. **Compare honestly.** Weigh stability, pay structure, location, growth speed, the work itself and personal goals; no option is right for everyone.

## Quality Checks

- [ ] Every stage is marked to confirm against the recruitment notice
- [ ] Eligibility requirements are checked against the person's background
- [ ] The question bank includes motivation, stability, teamwork and professional questions
- [ ] Answer points use the enterprise's real business, not generic praise
- [ ] Feedback gives a specific improvement for each dimension
- [ ] The offer comparison uses the person's own priorities

## Anti-Patterns

- **Generic answers about stability alone.** Interviewers want commitment to the work, not only to security.
- **Not knowing the business.** It is the most common way to fail an otherwise good interview.
- **Over-promising** on relocation or rotation the person would not accept.
- **Treating the choice as obvious.** State and private employers suit different goals.
