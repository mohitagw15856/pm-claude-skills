# Campus Recruitment (校招)

For Chinese graduates, campus recruitment is the main route into large companies and many public employers. It runs on a calendar: summer internships that can convert to offers, early batches (提前批), the main autumn season (秋招), and a smaller spring season (春招). Students lose out by starting late, sending one CV everywhere, or signing a tripartite agreement before comparing offers. This skill plans the season end to end.

Write in Simplified Chinese unless asked otherwise. Each company sets its own dates; confirm every date on its official campus recruitment site.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A season timeline**: internship applications, 提前批, 秋招, 补录 and 春招, with "confirm on the company site" markers
- **A tiered company list**: stretch, target and safer employers by industry and role
- **A one-page campus CV** focused on internships, projects, competitions and skills
- **Online application drafts (网申)**: answers to common open questions
- **A test and interview plan**: aptitude tests (测评 / 笔试), technical or case interviews, group interviews (群面), HR interviews
- **An offer comparison** and a checklist before signing the 三方协议

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **Degree, school, major and graduation date**
- **Target roles and industries**, and any target companies or cities
- **Experience**: internships, projects, competitions, student roles, papers
- **Current stage**: early planning, applications open, or holding offers
- **Constraints**: city, household registration (户口) needs, further study plans

## Framework

1. **Work back from the calendar.** Many large companies open summer internships in spring and convert interns to offers; early batches can open in summer; the main autumn season is busiest in September and October; spring recruitment fills remaining places. Treat these as typical patterns and confirm each company's dates.
2. **Tier the list.** About a third stretch, a third target, a third safer, spread across at least two industries.
3. **One page, results first.** Each item: what you did, how, and a result with a number. Tailor the order of sections to the role: projects first for technical roles, internships first for business roles.
4. **网申 answers are short stories.** For "describe a challenge" or "why us", use situation, action and result in 200 to 300 characters, specific to the company.
5. **Prepare by round.** Aptitude tests (practise timed), role tests (coding, case, writing), group interviews (contribute structure, keep time, build on others), professional interviews (projects in depth), HR interviews (motivation, stability, expectations).
6. **Compare before signing.** Compare offers on role, team, city, total pay, 户口 support, training and growth. Read the 三方协议 terms, including any breach penalty (违约金) and the deadline, before signing; ask the university careers office if unsure.

## Quality Checks

- [ ] Every date is marked to confirm on the company's official site
- [ ] The company list has stretch, target and safer tiers
- [ ] The CV fits one page and every item has an action and a result
- [ ] Application answers are specific to the named company
- [ ] The interview plan covers every round the target companies use
- [ ] The offer checklist covers the 三方协议 terms before signing

## Anti-Patterns

- **Starting in September.** By then many internship conversions and early batches are done.
- **One CV for every role.** Reorder and reword for each role family.
- **Signing the first offer.** Compare first; breaking a 三方协议 later can cost money and goodwill.
- **Silence in the group interview.** Structuring the discussion or keeping time counts.
