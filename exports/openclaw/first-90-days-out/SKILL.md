# First 90 Days Out

The first months after release decide a lot — and everything feels urgent at once with the fewest resources you'll have. This turns the chaos into a sequence: what to set up in week one, what follows, which deadlines carry real consequences (parole check-ins above all), and what can safely wait — plus where to get help — so the early days build a foundation instead of a crisis.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A week-by-week sequence** — the setup in the order it unlocks: parole/probation check-in, ID and vital documents, phone, benefits application, housing, bank account, health coverage and medications
- **The consequence deadlines** — the dates that carry real penalties (parole/probation reporting above all) flagged first and hardest
- **An urgent-vs-wait triage** — what genuinely must happen now vs. what can follow, so effort isn't scattered
- **The document chain** — how ID unlocks benefits, benefits unlock housing, etc., so it's tackled in an order that actually works
- **A support map** — reentry programs, parole/probation resources, case managers, and what each can do for you
- **A "when you're overwhelmed" reset** — the single next thing to do when it's too much

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **Your situation** — supervision status (parole/probation and its conditions), where you're staying day one
- **What you have** — any ID, documents, phone, money, support people
- **Immediate needs** — health/meds, a reporting date, a housing deadline
- **Where** — region (benefits and reentry services are local)

## Framework: Compliance First, Then the Document Chain

1. **Lock compliance first.** Parole/probation reporting and conditions come before everything — a missed check-in can undo it all. Put those dates at the top.
2. **Start the document chain.** ID is the key that unlocks benefits, housing, and work — begin it week one, because everything downstream waits on it.
3. **Sequence, don't scatter.** Benefits → housing → bank → health follow ID in a dependency order; doing them out of order stalls.
4. **Triage urgent vs. wait.** Health/meds and a roof are now; optimizing a résumé can wait a few weeks. Protect energy for what has consequences.
5. **Plug into support immediately.** A reentry case manager can shortcut half of this — connecting early multiplies everything else.
6. **Have an overwhelm reset.** When it's too much, do the one next compliance/document step; momentum beats the spiral.

## Output Format

### First 90 days: [supervision status] · [region]

**Compliance (do first):** [parole/probation reporting dates + conditions — non-negotiable].
**Week 1:** [ID/documents start · phone · report · immediate health/meds].
**Weeks 2–4:** [benefits application · housing · bank account].
**Weeks 5–12:** [health coverage · work search (→ job-search-with-a-record) · stability].
**Urgent vs. wait:** [now: X · later: Y].
**Support map:** [reentry program · case manager · parole resources — what each does].
**Overwhelmed? Do this one thing:** [the single next compliance/document step].

> Not legal advice — supervision conditions are set by your parole/probation officer and control. Confirm anything that affects compliance with them or a reentry legal service.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Puts parole/probation compliance above all else
- [ ] Starts the ID/document chain in week one
- [ ] Sequences benefits/housing/bank/health by dependency
- [ ] Triages urgent vs. what can wait
- [ ] Connects to reentry support early; includes an overwhelm reset

## Anti-Patterns
- **Burying the reporting deadline** under lower-stakes tasks.
- **Chasing housing or work** before the ID that unlocks them.
- **A flat to-do list** with no order or consequence-weighting.
- **Going it alone** instead of using a reentry case manager.
- **No plan for overwhelm**, so one hard day derails everything.

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "I'm getting out next month — what do I do first?"
- "Help me make a reentry plan for after release."
- "I just got out and I'm completely overwhelmed."
- "What are the first steps after prison — ID, benefits, all of it?"
- "Sequence the things I have to set up after incarceration."
