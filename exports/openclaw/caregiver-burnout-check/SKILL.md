# Caregiver-Burnout Check

Caregivers give until there's nothing left — and because the person they care for needs them, they ignore their own depletion until they collapse or get ill. That helps no one. This checks honestly how burnt out you are, gives you permission (and reasons) to accept help you've been refusing, and builds a realistic plan to sustain yourself — respite, support, and the guilt to address — because your wellbeing isn't selfish, it's the foundation the care depends on. Not treatment.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A burnout read** — an honest look at your signs (exhaustion, resentment, hopelessness, health decline, withdrawal, short fuse) and how depleted you are
- **Permission to accept help** — the reframe caregivers most need: that accepting help isn't failure or abandonment, and that you can't pour from empty
- **Concrete support & respite** — specific ways to get a break and share the load (respite care, other family, services, support groups) rather than "take time for yourself"
- **The guilt & identity traps** — addressing the guilt, the "only I can do this," and the identity fusion that keep caregivers from taking care of themselves
- **A realistic self-care plan** — small, doable ways to recover that fit a caregiver's actual constrained life, not idealized advice
- **A boundary** — a supportive check, not mental-health treatment; a flag to seek professional support if you're in serious distress

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **Your situation** — who you care for, how intensively, and for how long
- **How you're doing** — the signs you're noticing in yourself, honestly
- **The support you have** — other family, services, and what you've refused
- **What's stopping you** — guilt, "no one else can," no time, no money
- **How depleted** — coping but tired, or genuinely at the end of your rope

## Framework: See It, Accept Help, Sustain Yourself

1. **Name the burnout honestly.** Caregivers minimize their own state — reflect the signs back plainly (resentment and a short fuse are burnout, not being a bad person).
2. **Reframe accepting help.** The core block: accepting help feels like failing or abandoning them. Counter it — you can't sustain care while depleted; getting help *is* caring for them.
3. **Get concrete support.** Replace vague self-care with specific moves: respite care, dividing tasks with family, services, and a caregiver support group (which reduce isolation).
4. **Address the guilt and identity traps.** The "only I can do it," the guilt of a break, and losing yourself in the role — surface these gently, because they're what keep the burnout going.
5. **Build a realistic plan.** Recovery for a caregiver isn't a spa weekend — it's small, doable protections that fit a constrained life. Make it real.
6. **Flag serious distress.** If there's depression, hopelessness, or thoughts of not coping, gently point to professional support — this check isn't treatment.

## Output Format

### Caregiver check: caring for [who] · for [how long]

**Your burnout signs:** [honest reflection] → depletion level: [coping-but-tired / at the end of your rope].
**Accept help (the reframe):** [you can't pour from empty; getting help IS caring for them — not failure].
**Concrete support & respite:** [respite care · divide tasks with family · services · a support group].
**The traps keeping you stuck:** [guilt · "only I can do this" · losing yourself in the role].
**A realistic plan:** [small, doable protections that fit your actual life].

> A supportive check — not mental-health treatment. If you're feeling hopeless, depressed, or unable to cope, please reach out to a professional or a caregiver support line.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Reflects the burnout signs honestly (past the minimizing)
- [ ] Reframes accepting help as caring, not failing
- [ ] Gives concrete support/respite options, not vague "self-care"
- [ ] Addresses the guilt/identity traps that sustain burnout
- [ ] Builds a realistic plan that fits a caregiver's life
- [ ] Flags serious distress for professional help; not treatment

## Anti-Patterns
- **"Just take time for yourself"** — useless without concrete respite.
- **Not challenging** the "only I can do this" belief.
- **Guilt-free spa-weekend advice** that doesn't fit their life.
- **Minimizing** their depletion.
- **Playing therapist** on serious depression/hopelessness.

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "I'm exhausted caring for my mom — am I burning out?"
- "I have nothing left to give. Caregiving is destroying me."
- "I feel guilty even thinking about taking a break from caregiving."
- "How do I take care of myself while caring for my dad full-time?"
- "Caregiver stress is overwhelming me — help."
