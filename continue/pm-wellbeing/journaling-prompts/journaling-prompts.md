# Journaling Prompts

Blank-page journaling stalls fast. This gives you a focused, small set of prompts aimed at what you're actually here for — untangling a decision, processing something hard, chasing a goal, or just starting the habit — with a format light enough that you'll come back tomorrow.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A focused prompt set** — a handful of questions matched to your intent (not 50 generic ones)
- **A format & cadence** — how long, how often, morning vs evening, structured vs free
- **A beginner on-ramp** — the smallest possible starting point if you've never journaled
- **A depth ladder** — a couple of "go deeper" follow-ups for when a prompt opens something up
- **A gentle boundary** — encouragement to reflect, with a note that heavy or persistent distress is worth taking to a professional

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **Your intent** — decision, processing emotions, goal/growth, gratitude, or habit-building
- **The situation** — what's on your mind (as much or little as you want to share)
- **Experience** — new to journaling or regular
- **Time** — a few minutes or a longer sit
- **Preference** — structured questions vs open free-writing

## Framework: Focused, Light, Kind

1. **Match prompts to intent.** Decision-making, emotional processing, and goal work need different questions — target the person's actual reason.
2. **Keep the set small.** A few strong prompts beat an overwhelming list; the goal is to start writing, not to answer everything.
3. **Lower the bar for beginners.** Offer a tiny starter (three lines, one prompt) so the habit forms before ambition scales.
4. **Offer depth, don't force it.** Provide follow-up questions for when something surfaces, but let the person choose how far to go.
5. **Know the edge.** Reflection helps a lot; for trauma, persistent low mood, or crisis, gently suggest a professional — journaling isn't therapy.

## Output Format

### Journaling for: [intent] · [experience] · [time]

**Format:** [when/how long/structured vs free].

**Prompts**
1. [prompt] · 2. [prompt] · 3. [prompt] (+ a couple more tuned to intent).

**If you're just starting:** [one tiny prompt + 3 lines].
**Go deeper (optional):** [follow-up questions].

> Journaling is a great reflection tool. If you're dealing with heavy or ongoing distress, consider talking to a professional — this isn't a substitute for support.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Prompts match the stated intent, not generic
- [ ] The set is small and inviting, not overwhelming
- [ ] A beginner on-ramp is offered
- [ ] Optional depth follow-ups are included
- [ ] A kind boundary about professional support is present

## Anti-Patterns
- **A giant generic list** ("how was your day?" × 30).
- **Prompts mismatched to intent** — gratitude questions for someone facing a hard decision.
- **Too much at once** for a beginner.
- **Pushing depth** the person didn't ask for.
- **Playing therapist** on heavy topics instead of suggesting real support.

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "Give me journaling prompts to help me decide about a job offer."
- "I want to start journaling but never know what to write."
- "Prompts for processing a breakup."
- "Morning journaling prompts for motivation and goals."
- "Help me reflect on a stressful week."
