# Sponsor Read Script Skill

A sponsor read works when it sounds like the person the audience came for and fails when it sounds like a brochure read aloud. It also has rules: the audience must be told it is an advertisement, and the creator is responsible for any claim they voice. This skill writes the read in the creator's voice from the sponsor's brief, keeps the required points, and removes claims the creator cannot stand behind. To judge whether a deal is worth taking, use `creator-deal-decoder`; to pitch sponsors, use `creator-media-kit`.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A short read** (about 30 seconds) and **a long read** (about 60 seconds), with a word count for each
- **The disclosure line**, placed at the start
- **A coverage checklist**: each required point from the brief and where it appears
- **Claims to cut or check**: anything unsubstantiated, absolute or outside the creator's own experience
- **The call to action**, with the code or link exactly as supplied

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The sponsor's brief**: product, required talking points, mandatory wording, things not to say, the code or link
- **The creator's real experience** with the product. If they have not used it, the read must not imply they have
- **A sample of how the creator talks**: a transcript excerpt or a few past lines
- **Format and length**: video, podcast or newsletter, and the seconds or words allowed
- **Where the audience is**, since disclosure rules differ by country and platform

Use the discount code, link and product claims exactly as given. Never invent a price, a discount, a statistic or a personal story. Tell the creator to check their platform's and country's current disclosure rules; describe the principle (say clearly, early, that it is paid) and do not quote a regulation from memory.

## Framework: Their Points, Your Voice

1. **Disclose first.** One plain sentence that this part is sponsored, before the pitch begins.
2. **Open with a true connection.** A real problem the creator or their audience has that the product addresses. If there is no honest connection, say so to the creator before writing.
3. **Say what it is in one line**, in the creator's words, not the brief's.
4. **Give one specific reason**, drawn from the creator's own use where it exists. One concrete detail persuades more than three adjectives.
5. **Cover the required points** without reading them as a list.
6. **State the offer and the action** once, clearly: the code or link, exactly as supplied, and what the listener gets.
7. **Remove what cannot be stood behind.** Superlatives, health or money promises, comparisons with competitors, and anything the creator has not seen for themselves go to the check list.
8. **Budget the words.** Spoken reads run at roughly two and a half words a second.

## Output Format

# Sponsor Read: [sponsor] for [show or channel]

**Disclosure:** "[the line]"

## Short Read (about 30 seconds, [N] words)
> [script]

## Long Read (about 60 seconds, [N] words)
> [script]

## Required Points
| Point from the brief | Covered in | Wording used |
|----------------------|------------|--------------|

## Cut or Check Before Recording
| Claim in the brief | Why | What to do |
|--------------------|-----|------------|

## Call to Action
[Code or link exactly as supplied · what the audience gets]

## Quality Checks

- [ ] The disclosure comes before the pitch and is unmistakable
- [ ] No personal experience is claimed that the creator did not report
- [ ] The code, link, price and offer match the brief exactly
- [ ] Every required point is covered, and the table shows where
- [ ] Unsupported or absolute claims are listed for checking, not voiced
- [ ] Word counts fit the time allowed

## Anti-Patterns

- [ ] Do not bury or soften the disclosure
- [ ] Do not write "I use this every day" unless the creator said so
- [ ] Do not read the brief's bullet points aloud
- [ ] Do not make health, financial or guaranteed-result claims on the sponsor's say-so
- [ ] Do not invent a discount or a deadline
