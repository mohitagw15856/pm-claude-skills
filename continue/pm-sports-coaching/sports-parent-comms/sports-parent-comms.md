# Sports Parent Comms

Most friction in youth sport is about communication, not coaching: unclear arrangements, surprises about selection, and a tense reply sent late at night. This skill writes messages that are short, consistent with the team's stated approach, and safe to forward. It also says when a message is the wrong tool.

## What This Skill Produces

- The message, sized for the channel (group chat, email, club app)
- For difficult topics: a first reply that acknowledges and proposes a conversation, and a fuller follow-up
- A consistency check against the team's playing-time and selection policy
- A safeguarding and privacy check
- A note on anything that should be said in person

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:

- Type of message: welcome, logistics, selection, behaviour, complaint reply, injury, cancellation
- Team, age group and the club's or school's policies on playing time, selection and conduct
- The facts: dates, times, venue, kit, who is affected
- For a complaint: what the parent said, what actually happened, and what the coach is willing to offer
- Channel and whether the message goes to one family or the whole group
- The club's safeguarding rules on contacting players and sharing photos

## Framework

1. **One purpose per message.** Logistics and a behaviour reminder do not share a message.
2. **Logistics in a fixed order.** What, when, where, bring, reply by.
3. **Selection and playing time are explained by policy, not by comparison.** Never discuss another child. State the approach, what the player is working on, and when it will be reviewed.
4. **Group for information, individual for anything personal.** Nothing about one child goes to the group.
5. **Complaints: acknowledge, do not argue in text, offer a time to talk.** Reply once, briefly, within a day. Not on match day.
6. **Behaviour reminders address everyone and describe the behaviour wanted.**
7. **Safeguarding.** Messages go to parents or carers, not privately to children. No injury details or photos without consent. Concerns about a child's welfare go to the club's safeguarding officer, not into a message.

## Output Format

### [Message type] for [audience], [channel]

**Message**
> [Text ready to send.]

**If this is a difficult topic**
- **Short first reply:** [two or three sentences]
- **Fuller follow-up after the conversation:** [text]

**Checks**
- Policy consistency: [matches the stated policy / differs because ...]
- Privacy and safeguarding: [nothing about an individual child in a group message, addressed to parents]
- Better said in person: [yes or no, and why]

**Assumptions and gaps**
- [Policy or facts assumed]
- [What to confirm before sending]

## Quality Checks

- [ ] The message has one purpose
- [ ] Logistics include time, place, what to bring and a reply-by date
- [ ] No other child is named or compared
- [ ] Complaint replies acknowledge the concern and offer a conversation
- [ ] The message would be fine if forwarded to the club chair

## Anti-Patterns

- **Do not justify selection by criticising a child.**
- **Do not reply to a complaint in the group chat.**
- **Do not send a behaviour reminder that singles out one family in public.**
- **Do not message a child directly** where club rules require contact through parents.
- **Do not promise playing time** you cannot deliver.

## Example Trigger Phrases

- "Write the welcome message for the new season for under-10 parents."
- "A dad is angry his daughter only played ten minutes. Draft my reply."
- "Remind parents about shouting from the touchline without sounding preachy."
- "Saturday's match is off because of the pitch. Tell the group."
