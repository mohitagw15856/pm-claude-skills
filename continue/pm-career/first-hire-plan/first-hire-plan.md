# First-Hire Plan

Your first hire is a big, nervy step — get the role wrong or the classification wrong and it's costly. This helps you decide whether you're ready, what to hand off first, and whether a contractor or employee fits — then gives a simple, fair hiring process for someone who's never done it, plus the obligations to know about. It flags that employment, tax, and legal rules are local and this isn't legal advice.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A readiness & role read** — whether now's the time, and which work to offload first (the highest-leverage or most-draining tasks)
- **Contractor vs. employee** — the trade-offs for your situation (cost, control, commitment, classification rules) and a recommendation
- **A lightweight hiring process** — defining the role, sourcing candidates, a fair and simple evaluation, and making an offer
- **Obligations awareness** — the tax, payroll, insurance, and legal responsibilities a first hire triggers (flagged to verify locally)
- **Onboarding basics** — setting the person up to succeed in the first weeks
- **A boundary** — employment/tax/legal specifics are local; consult a professional

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The business** — what you do, size, and where you're stretched
- **The need** — what work you'd hand off, and how much of it
- **Budget & commitment** — what you can afford, and how steady the need is
- **Contractor or employee leaning** — any preference or constraint
- **Location** — for the (varying) employment/tax obligations

## Framework: Right Time, Right Role, Right Classification

1. **Check readiness and pick the role.** Confirm the workload and budget justify a hire, then define the highest-leverage thing to hand off first — don't hire a vague "help."
2. **Decide contractor vs. employee honestly.** Weigh cost, control, commitment, and — critically — classification rules (misclassifying an employee as a contractor is a real risk). Recommend the fit.
3. **Define the role clearly.** A concrete role description (responsibilities, must-haves, success in 90 days) makes sourcing and evaluation fair and effective.
4. **Run a simple, fair process.** Source candidates, evaluate on relevant signals (a small work sample beats gut feel), and make a clear offer.
5. **Know the obligations.** A first hire can trigger payroll, tax withholding, insurance, and legal duties — flag these to set up correctly, verified locally.
6. **Onboard deliberately.** Set expectations, tools, and early wins so the hire actually reduces your load.

## Anti-Patterns
- **Hiring a vague "helper"** with no defined role.
- **Misclassifying an employee as a contractor** to cut cost.
- **Gut-feel hiring** with no work sample or clear criteria.
- **Ignoring payroll/tax/legal obligations.**
- **No onboarding** — the hire flounders and doesn't reduce load.
