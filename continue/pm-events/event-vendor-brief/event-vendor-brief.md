# Event Vendor Brief

Three quotes that cannot be compared is a briefing failure, not a supplier one. Each priced a different event because each was told a different amount. This writes the brief that fixes the variables — outcome, constraints, deliverables, and the exact response format — so the quotes come back on the same basis and the cheapest one is actually the cheapest.

## What This Skill Produces

- **The outcome brief** — what the supplier is being asked to achieve, not just to provide
- **Deliverables and constraints** — the specifics that drive price: quantities, timings, access, and restrictions
- **A quote-response template** — the structure every supplier must return, which is what makes bids comparable
- **Evaluation criteria with weights** — stated up front, including how price is weighted against everything else
- **The questions each supplier must answer** — capability, capacity, contingency, and what they need from you
- **The information they need from you** — venue details, access, timings, and contacts, supplied once rather than repeatedly

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The event** — type, date, venue, headcount, format, and the feel you are aiming for
- **The scope for this supplier** — what you want them to cover, and what someone else is covering
- **The constraints** — budget range, venue restrictions, access times, power, noise limits, curfew
- **The deadline** — when quotes are needed and when you will decide
- **What matters most** — price, reliability, creativity, sustainability, or experience with this venue

## Framework: Brief the Outcome, Fix the Variables, Standardise the Response

1. **Brief the outcome first.** 'Guests should be able to hold a conversation while eating' tells a supplier more than a speaker specification, and lets them bring expertise you do not have.
2. **Then fix the variables that move price.** Headcount, duration, service style, access window, and get-out time. Any of these left vague and every quote will assume differently.
3. **Give a budget range.** Withholding it produces quotes aimed at an imagined number and wastes two rounds. A range is not a weakness.
4. **Mandate the response format.** Same line items, same order, same inclusions and exclusions. This single step is what makes comparison possible.
5. **State the evaluation criteria and weights.** Suppliers respond to what is measured, and it keeps your own decision honest later.
6. **Ask what they need from you.** The good suppliers answer this well, and it surfaces your own gaps early.
7. **Send the same brief to everyone, at the same time.** Otherwise you are comparing responses to different questions.

## Output Format

### Supplier brief: [discipline] · [event] · [date]

**Event:** [type, date, venue, headcount, format] · **The feel:** [in a sentence]

**Outcome sought:** [what success looks like from the guest's point of view]

**Scope**
- In scope for you: [list]
- Covered by others: [list, and who]

**Constraints:** venue [access window, load-in route, lift, power available, noise limit, curfew] · budget range [amount–amount] · [other]

**Required response format** — please return exactly these headings
1. Approach — [how you would achieve the outcome]
2. Itemised costs — [line, quantity, unit, total] with **inclusions and exclusions stated separately**
3. Crew — [number, roles, arrival and departure times]
4. Equipment/materials — [list]
5. Contingency — [what you do if X fails]
6. What you need from us — [access, power, information, decisions and by when]
7. References — [two comparable events]

**Evaluation:** [criterion — weight]% each, totalling 100

**Dates:** questions by [date] · quotes by [date] · decision [date] · event [date]

**Contact:** [name, email, phone]

## Quality Checks
- [ ] The outcome is briefed before the specification
- [ ] Every price-moving variable is fixed: headcount, duration, service style, access, get-out
- [ ] A budget range is given
- [ ] A mandatory response format is specified so bids arrive comparable
- [ ] Evaluation criteria and weights are stated up front
- [ ] Suppliers are asked what they need from you
- [ ] The identical brief went to every supplier at the same time

## Anti-Patterns
- **Specifying equipment instead of outcome.** Buys you the supplier's stock list, not their expertise.
- **Withholding the budget.** Costs two rounds of quoting and produces irrelevant proposals.
- **No mandated response format.** Guarantees incomparable quotes and a decision made on presentation quality.
- **Leaving access and get-out vague.** The two largest hidden cost drivers in event supply.
- **Different briefs to different suppliers.** Whatever you learn from the comparison is noise.
- **Not asking what they need from you.** The gaps surface on the day instead.
- **Choosing on headline price.** The exclusions are where the difference actually is.
