# Farm Buyer Contract Check

Contracts between farmers and buyers are usually written by the buyer. The risk rarely sits in the headline price. It sits in who grades the crop, what is deducted, when payment arrives and what the farmer owes after a failed harvest. This skill reads the contract from the grower's side and shows what it means in money. It is not legal advice; for a contract of any size, a lawyer or a farmers' organisation should read it too.

## What This Skill Produces

- A plain-language summary of what each side has promised
- A clause-by-clause table: what it says, what it means for the farmer, risk level
- A worked example of the net payment in a good season and a poor one
- Questions to ask the buyer and specific changes to request
- A walk-away list: the terms that should stop the farmer signing as written

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:

- The contract text, or the key terms as the farmer understands them
- Crop or product, area or volume, season covered
- The farmer's expected yield, cost of production and the current open-market price
- Any inputs, credit or services the buyer provides, and their price
- Who else buys this crop locally
- The country, so that mandatory protections can be flagged for checking

## Framework: Ten Places the Risk Hides

1. **Price.** Fixed, formula or "market price on the day". Who publishes the reference price, and on which date?
2. **Quality and grading.** Written grade standards, who grades, where, and whether the farmer can be present or appeal.
3. **Rejection.** What happens to rejected produce, who pays transport back, and whether the farmer may sell it elsewhere.
4. **Volume.** Must the farmer deliver a minimum? Must the buyer take everything? A one-sided obligation is a warning sign.
5. **Deductions.** Inputs, interest, transport, levies, moisture, weight loss. Ask for every deduction to be listed with a rate.
6. **Payment timing.** Days after delivery, method, and the remedy for late payment.
7. **Input credit.** Price of supplied inputs against the market price, interest, and what is owed if the crop fails.
8. **Exclusivity and side-selling.** Penalties for selling elsewhere, and whether they apply to surplus.
9. **Bad season.** Drought, flood, disease: is the farmer excused, or still in debt?
10. **Disputes and exit.** Who decides, where, at whose cost, and how either side can end the contract.

## Quality Checks

- [ ] All ten risk areas are addressed, or marked "not in the contract"
- [ ] The worked example uses the farmer's own yield and cost figures
- [ ] Silent clauses are flagged as risks, not ignored
- [ ] Each requested change has wording the farmer can propose
- [ ] No statement about national law is made without telling the farmer to verify it

## Anti-Patterns

- **Do not judge the contract on the headline price alone.**
- **Do not assume missing terms favour the farmer.**
- **Do not state what the law requires in a given country** without marking it for verification.
- **Do not advise side-selling** in breach of a signed contract.
- **Do not soften a walk-away finding.**

## Example Trigger Phrases

- "A company wants me to grow chillies on contract. Is this agreement fair?"
- "What does 'price determined at factory gate after grading' mean for me?"
- "They supply seed on credit. What do I owe if the crop fails?"
- "The buyer rejected a third of my delivery. What does the contract let me do?"
