# 小宇宙播客提纲

A Chinese podcast episode is long, conversational and listened to on commutes and at the gym. Listeners choose from the title and the show notes, skip by the timeline, and comment at specific timestamps. An episode with no plan wanders; one that is over-scripted sounds read. This skill plans the conversation and the packaging without scripting the talk itself.

Write in Simplified Chinese.

## What This Skill Produces

- Three episode titles and a one-line hook
- An episode brief: angle, listener, what they will take away
- A timed outline: segments, main questions, follow-ups, stories to draw out
- A cold open and a standard intro
- A guest briefing note
- Show notes: summary, guest introduction, timeline, references, credits
- Sponsor read placement and a disclosure check

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:

- The show's theme, hosts and usual tone
- Episode topic and why now
- Format: solo, co-host chat, interview, panel
- Guest: who they are, what only they can say, anything off limits
- Target length
- Material: articles, books, data, personal stories to draw on
- Sponsor or commercial arrangement, if any
- Where it will be published and whether video is recorded

## Framework

1. **Angle.** One question the episode answers or one tension it explores. A topic is not an angle.
2. **Title.** Concrete and curious. The guest's name helps only if the audience knows them. Avoid numbering alone.
3. **Cold open.** Thirty to sixty seconds from the best moment of the recording, or a short set-up that poses the question, before the theme music.
4. **Segments.** Four to six, each with a purpose, a main question and two or three follow-ups. Open with something easy and specific; put the hardest question after trust is built; end with a forward-looking or personal question.
5. **Questions.** Open questions that ask for a story or a decision: "那次你是怎么决定的?" not "你觉得重要吗?". Prepare follow-ups: "具体是什么样的?", "后来呢?", "有没有相反的例子?".
6. **Timing.** Give each segment a target. Mark what can be cut if the conversation runs long.
7. **Guest briefing.** Topic and angle, the segments in outline, what they do not need to prepare, recording logistics, and what will be edited. Do not send a word-for-word script.
8. **Show notes.** Two or three sentences on the episode, the guest's introduction, a timeline with timestamps and segment headings, books and links mentioned, production credits, how to reach the show. The timeline is filled in after editing.
9. **Sponsor reads.** Place at a natural break, in the host's voice, clearly identified as sponsored.
10. **Rights and consent.** Music licences, permission for clips, and the guest's consent to publication and editing.

## Quality Checks

- [ ] The angle is a question or tension, not a subject label
- [ ] Each segment has a purpose and open questions
- [ ] The cold open is under a minute
- [ ] Cuts are marked for an over-long recording
- [ ] Show notes include a timeline structure and references
- [ ] Sponsored content is identified

## Anti-Patterns

- **Do not script the conversation word for word.**
- **Do not start with five minutes of small talk.**
- **Do not ask questions the guest has answered in every other interview** without a new angle.
- **Do not invent the guest's views in the brief.**
- **Do not leave the timeline out of the show notes.**
