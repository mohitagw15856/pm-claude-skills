# Money Mindset Reset

Personal finance is only half math — the other half is the beliefs and emotions you absorbed about money, usually before you can remember. They run quietly: the avoider who won't open bills, the overspender soothing something, the scarcity-gripped saver who can't enjoy anything, the guilt around wanting more. This surfaces your money story, names the beliefs sabotaging you, and resets toward a healthier relationship — with small behavior shifts. Not therapy, not financial advice.

## What This Skill Produces

- **Your money story** — where your beliefs about money came from (family, early experiences, culture) and how they still run
- **The sabotaging beliefs** — the specific scripts driving unhelpful behavior (avoidance, overspending, scarcity, money guilt, "I'm bad with money")
- **The behavior link** — how each belief shows up in what you actually do with money
- **A healthier reframe** — a more accurate, useful stance to replace each unhelpful belief
- **Small behavior shifts** — the tiny concrete changes that follow the reframe (facing the numbers, a values-based spending rule, permission to enjoy)
- **A boundary** — supportive reframing, not therapy; a nudge to real support if money issues tie to deeper distress

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The pattern** — what you do with money that frustrates you (avoid, overspend, hoard, panic)
- **Your money story** — what money was like growing up, and messages you absorbed
- **The feeling** — what money brings up (anxiety, guilt, shame, fear, numbness)
- **Your goal** — what a healthier relationship with money would look like for you

## Framework: Surface The Story, Reframe, Shift

1. **Trace the story.** Money beliefs are learned early — surface where the person's came from, because you can't change a script you can't see.
2. **Name the sabotaging belief.** Connect the pattern to the belief underneath it ("money is scarce and dangerous" → hoarding and anxiety; "I don't deserve it" → self-sabotage).
3. **Link belief to behavior.** Show how the belief produces the specific financial behavior — making the invisible driver visible.
4. **Reframe accurately.** Replace the distorted belief with a truer, more useful one — not toxic positivity, but a grounded shift ("I can learn this," "money is a tool, not a verdict").
5. **Shift the behavior small.** One concrete change that flows from the reframe — facing the bank balance, a values-aligned spending choice, permission to spend on what matters.
6. **Hold the boundary.** This is supportive reframing, not therapy; if money distress ties to trauma, anxiety, or compulsion, gently point to real support.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Traces the money story to its origins
- [ ] Names the specific sabotaging belief
- [ ] Links the belief to the actual financial behavior
- [ ] Offers a grounded reframe (not toxic positivity)
- [ ] Gives one small concrete behavior shift
- [ ] Holds a not-therapy boundary and points to support if needed

## Anti-Patterns
- **Pure math advice** ignoring the emotional driver.
- **Toxic positivity** ("just think abundant!") instead of a grounded reframe.
- **Shaming** the person for their money behavior.
- **A big overhaul** instead of one small shift.
- **Playing therapist** on deeper distress.

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "I have a terrible relationship with money — help me reset it."
- "Why do I keep self-sabotaging financially?"
- "Money stresses me out and I avoid dealing with it."
- "I earn fine but always feel broke and anxious about money."
- "Help me fix my money mindset — I feel guilt around spending."
