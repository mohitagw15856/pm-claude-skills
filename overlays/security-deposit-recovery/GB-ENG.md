## In England (assured shorthold tenancies)
- The landlord must protect the deposit in a government-approved scheme (Deposit Protection Service, MyDeposits or the Tenancy Deposit Scheme) within 30 days of receiving it, and give the tenant the scheme's prescribed information. Ask which scheme holds it; each has a search page.
- If it was not protected on time, the tenant can apply to court for compensation of between one and three times the deposit.
- Each scheme offers a free dispute resolution service. Both sides must agree to use it, and its decision is usually final, so send the evidence (check-in and check-out inventories, photos, receipts) to the scheme rather than arguing only with the landlord.
- For most tenancies since 1 June 2019, the deposit is capped at five weeks' rent where annual rent is under £50,000 (six weeks at £50,000 or more).
- Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have different rules; do not apply these there.
