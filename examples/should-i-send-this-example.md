# Example: should-i-send-this

## What this skill does

Gut-checks a message before you send it: how it will land for the recipient, where emotion is leaking, misreadable lines and overshares, whether now is the right time, a calmer rewrite if needed, and a clear verdict.

## How to trigger it

### Example 1: the hot reply to a colleague

**You say:**
> "Check this before I hit send. To my colleague Raj, who presented my analysis as his own in the leadership meeting an hour ago: 'Interesting presentation today. Funny how slide 8 looked exactly like the model I shared with you last week. Next time maybe mention where it came from? Or I can mention it myself.'"

### The message: calling out credit-taking · to Raj · you feel angry (an hour old)

**How it'll land for them:** as a threat, specifically the last line. "Or I can mention it myself" reads as "I will escalate", and whatever legitimate grievance sits above it, the recipient will remember only the threat. "Funny how" is sarcasm; sarcasm in writing lands as contempt.

**Risk flags:**
- Emotion leak: the whole message is written at the anger's temperature; it is one hour old.
- Misreadable: "Interesting presentation" opener will be re-read as mockery once they reach line two.
- Regret: the threat line creates a record that looks bad *for you* if this ever does escalate.

**Timing:** wait. You lose nothing by sending tomorrow morning; you lose options by sending now. The facts will still be true; the sarcasm won't be in them.

**Calmer version (if needed):**
> "Raj, the model on slide 8 was the one I shared with you last week. I'd have liked it credited, and I'd like it corrected with the group before the deck circulates. Can you do that this week, or should we do it together?"

**Verdict:** wait an hour minimum, then send the rewrite. It keeps every ounce of the point (the claim, the ask, the deadline, a consequence) and loses the parts that would have become the story.

### Example 2: the 11pm overshare to a new friend

**You say:**
> "Is this okay to send? Long message to someone I met 3 weeks ago, explaining my whole history with my ex and why I'm 'like this'. It's 11pm and I've had wine."

**How it'll land for them:** a 3-week friendship receiving a chapter it hasn't earned yet; most people meet unrequested depth with polite retreat.

**Risk flags:** overshare you cannot unshare, sent from a wine-and-11pm state that is making intimacy look like urgency.

**Timing:** this is a message to *write* tonight and *decide about* at noon tomorrow. Save it to drafts; the writing already did most of what you needed.

**Verdict:** don't send tonight. If noon-tomorrow-you still wants the conversation, have it in person, smaller, and let it be a dialogue instead of a delivery.

## Tips for best results

- Say who it's to and what you're feeling; the same words carry differently to a boss, an ex, or a friend.
- Paste the real draft, not a summary; tone lives in the exact words.
- The verdict "wait" is not "don't"; it is a test the message passes by still seeming right later.
