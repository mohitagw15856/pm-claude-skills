# Example: Functional Assessment Prep

## What this skill does
Helps someone describe how their condition affects daily life honestly and specifically, with the evidence organised, before a disability benefit assessment.

## How to trigger it

### Example 1: Fluctuating condition
**You say:**
> "I have ME and a benefit assessment by phone next week. Some days I can cook, most days I can't get out of bed. Last time they said I was fine."

**Claude produces:**
**1. How the assessment works**
Assessors usually look at whether you can do each activity reliably, repeatedly, safely and in a reasonable time. Doing something once, then needing days to recover, usually does not count as being able to do it.

**2. Your day, in your words**
| Activity | Typical day | Bad day | Effect afterwards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preparing a meal | Microwave meals only, sitting down | Cannot stand long enough; partner cooks | A short cook leaves me in bed the next day |
| Washing | Shower once a week with a seat | Wash at the sink | Need to lie down for an hour |

Say how many days a week are bad, for example "four or five", and describe a bad day first.

---

### Example 2: After the assessment
**You say:**
> "The report says I walked to the room without difficulty. That's not true."

**Claude produces:**
Ask for a copy of the full report in writing. Note each statement you disagree with and what actually happened ("I used the wall for support and had to sit after 10 metres"). Check the deadline to ask for the decision to be looked at again; it is often short, around a month *(assumed, confirm)*. A welfare rights adviser can help for free.

---

## Tips for best results
- Describe your worst days and how often they happen, not just a typical day
- List every letter or report you have, even old ones
- Say whether the assessment is in person, by phone or by video

## Related skills
- `disability-benefit-appeal`: challenge a decision you disagree with
- `accommodation-request`: adjustments at work
