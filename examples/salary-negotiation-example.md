# Example: salary-negotiation

## What this skill does

Plans a compensation negotiation on numbers and leverage: total-comp comparison across offers, your target, walk-away and anchor, a leverage read, value-based justification, exact counter scripts including non-base levers, and the walk-away plan.

## How to trigger it

### Example 1: countering a single offer

**You say:**
> "Offer: £72k base, 10% bonus, £15k options over 4 years, from [scale-up]. I'm on £68k + 8% now, comfortable but under-levelled. Market for the role reads £75-85k. I want to counter but hate negotiating."

**Claude produces (excerpt):**

### Negotiation Plan: Senior role at [scale-up]

**1. Total-comp comparison:** offer ≈ £83k/year (72 + 7.2 bonus + ~3.75 options) vs current ≈ £73.4k. A 13% lift, before negotiating. Worth knowing so you negotiate from "good offer, make it right" rather than fear.

**2. Your numbers:** target £80k base; walk-away £74k base (below that, the move stops being worth switching risk); anchor £82k. All three sit inside your own market read, which is what makes them sayable out loud.

**3. Leverage read:** moderate. No competing offer, but they have spent four interview rounds on you, and your current job is a genuine BATNA; you can stay. Use the second, never bluff the first.

**4. The justification:** "I'm excited about this and want to say yes. Based on the market for this scope (£75-85k) and the [specific capability] I'm bringing, I'd like to get base to £82k. Can we make that work?" One sentence of evidence, one number, one question. Then stop talking; the silence is theirs to fill.

**5. Counter scripts:**
- To "that's our max on base": "Understood. Then let's look at the other levers: would a £6k sign-on and a 6-month review with a defined path to £80k work?"
- Non-base levers, in your preference order: sign-on (easiest yes), guaranteed review date, extra options, title/level (which compounds forever), start date.

**6. The walk-away plan:** below £74k base with no structured path up, you decline warmly and stay put. Deciding this now is the whole trick; people negotiate badly when any outcome must be yes.

### Example 2: two offers, rankings that flip

**You say:**
> "Compare: Offer A £78k base, no bonus, remote. Offer B £70k base + 20% bonus + £40k equity over 4, hybrid 3 days, 50-min commute."

**Claude produces (excerpt):**

**1. Total-comp comparison:** A ≈ £78k. B ≈ £94k on paper (70 + 14 + 10), *if* the bonus pays fully and the equity is worth face value; discount both (bonus at 70% history unknown, private equity at 25-50%) and B's realistic range is £77-89k. The ranking flips within your own discount assumptions, so the negotiation is actually for *information*: B's bonus payout history for the last 2 years, and the equity's last valuation. Ask for both before countering either.

**The commute, priced:** 3 days × 100 min ≈ 230 hours/year; at your effective rate that is £8-10k of unpaid time. A's remote is worth that much base. Say it as a fact, not a complaint, when countering B.

## Tips for best results

- Give total current comp and the market range you believe; targets anchored to nothing collapse under one pushback.
- Rehearse the counter script aloud once; the plan's value is having exact words when your pulse is up.
- Decide the walk-away before the call, in writing; it is the only number that protects you.
