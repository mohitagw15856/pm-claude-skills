# Example: five-minds

## What this skill does

Answers one question as five independent minds with clashing worldviews, each fully committed and unhedged, then names the tensions and synthesises what survives.

## How to trigger it

### Example 1: a career fork from every angle

**You say:**
> "Look at this from every angle: I'm a senior engineer, 34, offered an engineering manager role. Same pay, bigger scope. I like coding but feel plateaued."

**Claude produces (excerpt):**

### Question: take the EM role?

**🟢 The Ambition mind:** Take it. Scope compounds; code skills plateau precisely because the next level of impact is other people. You already feel the ceiling; this is what the ceiling feels like.

**🔴 The Craft mind:** Refuse. "Plateaued" at 34 means bored, not finished; the cure is harder technical problems, not calendars. Management will consume the hours that would un-plateau you, and the way back to deep technical work gets harder every year you're away.

**🔵 The Market mind:** The EM path is more replaceable than it looks: management roles are cut first in downturns, and staff-level ICs now out-earn line EMs at many companies. Same pay for more meetings is a worse trade than it was five years ago.

**🟠 The Systems mind:** The question is misposed as identity when it is an experiment. Most companies allow a return lane within 12 months; the real variables are whether this one does, and whether your successor as senior engineer would be hired before the lane closes.

**🟣 The Mortality mind:** You will spend ~2,000 hours next year on whichever you pick. Picture the Tuesday afternoon of each. The role you'd pick for the obituary and the one you'd pick for Tuesday are often different; trust Tuesday.

**Where they clash:** Ambition and Craft disagree on what "plateaued" is evidence of: readiness or boredom. That is the crux, and only you can say which it feels like from inside.

**Synthesis:** Take it only with a tested return lane (Systems), because the Market mind's asymmetry is real; and decide what "plateaued" means first, because if Craft is right, the EM role treats the wrong disease. Genuine tension remains between Ambition and Mortality; no synthesis dissolves it.

### Example 2: widening a product debate stuck on two options

**You say:**
> "We've argued build-vs-buy for payments for a month. Give me a range of views, not one answer."

**Claude produces (excerpt):**

**🟢 The Founder mind:** Buy. Payments is undifferentiated plumbing; every week spent there is a week your actual product stands still.
**🔴 The Margin mind:** Build. At your volume, 2.9% + 30p is your single largest COGS line in three years; the buy decision is a loan at compounding interest.
**🔵 The Risk mind:** Neither of you has priced compliance; building means PCI DSS Level 1, which doubles the build estimate on its own.

**Where they clash:** Founder and Margin are both right on different clocks (18 months vs 5 years). The decision is which clock the company is actually on.

## Tips for best results

- Give the real stakes and constraints; committed takes need material to commit to.
- Let the minds stay unblended; the hedge-free versions reveal more than a balanced summary would.
- Argue back at the mind you hate most; that reaction is usually the finding.

## Related skills

- `decision-panel` when you want a chair's verdict and a tie-breaker, not just the range
- `the-third-answer` when the problem is generic answers rather than too few perspectives
