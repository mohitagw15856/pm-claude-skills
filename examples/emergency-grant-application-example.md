# Example: Emergency Grant Application

## What this skill does
Finds emergency money in order of speed after a disaster or crisis, and prepares a clear statement of need for each application.

## How to trigger it

### Example 1: After a flood
**You say:**
> "Our home flooded last week. We're staying with family, we lost the kids' beds and the fridge, and my husband can't work while we sort it out. Is there emergency money?"

**Claude produces:**
**1. Where to apply, in order**
1. **Your council or municipality's emergency or hardship support** this week: often the fastest, for essentials like beds and appliances.
2. **National disaster assistance,** if the flood was declared a disaster in your area. Register early; there is usually a deadline, and you can often register before every paper is ready.
3. **Charity crisis grants and local foundations** for items not covered.
4. **Your husband's employer or union**: many have hardship or benevolent funds.

**3. Your statement**
"On 9 October our home at [address] flooded. We are staying with relatives. We lost two children's beds (about 300) and our fridge (about 400). My husband cannot work while we deal with the damage. We are asking for 700 for beds and a fridge so the children have somewhere to sleep when we return."

---

### Example 2: Refused
**You say:**
> "The hardship fund said no because we have savings."

**Claude produces:**
Ask for the decision and the savings limit in writing. If your savings are already committed (for example the insurance excess or temporary rent), show that with receipts and ask for a review.

---

## Tips for best results
- Say what happened, when, and exactly what you need and how much
- Mention children, disability or age in the household
- List any applications you have already made
