# Example: task-to-first-step

## What this skill does

Shrinks a task you're avoiding to a first step so small it's almost impossible not to do: the tiny first physical action, why it lowers the barrier, permission to stop after it, and the next micro-steps queued.

## How to trigger it

### Example 1: the report that's been on the list for two weeks

**You say:**
> "I can't get started on the annual performance self-review. It's been on my list for two weeks and I open the form and close it again."

**Claude produces (excerpt):**

### Task: the annual self-review

**👉 The only first step:** open the form and type one bullet under "achievements": the most recent thing you shipped, in plain words, badly phrased. ~2 minutes.

**Why this works:** it is physical (typing one line), immediate (the most recent thing needs no digging through the year), and decision-free (you are not choosing the *best* achievement, just the latest one; ranking comes never or later). The thing you are avoiding is not the form, it is summarising a year; this step does not require that.

**You can stop after that.** (You probably won't; the second bullet is easier than the first, because the page is no longer blank.)

**If you're rolling, next:**
2. Scroll your sent mail from one month, March, and copy anything that looks like a win into the doc, unedited.
3. Repeat for one more month. · 4. Stop collecting; bold the three that matter.

### Example 2: a task that's actually a decision in disguise

**You say:**
> "Break this down so I can start: 'sort out the website'. I've avoided it for a month."

**Claude produces (excerpt):**

### Task: "sort out the website"

**First, the honest read:** this task resists starting because it is not a task; it is an unmade decision wearing a to-do's clothes. "Sort out" hides: redesign? fix the copy? move hosts? Nobody can start a decision by doing.

**👉 The only first step:** open the site, look at the homepage for 60 seconds, and write down the single thing that annoys you most. ~2 minutes.

**Why this works:** it converts "sort out" into one named defect, and a named defect is startable. You are not fixing anything yet; you are only allowed to complain, in writing, once.

**If you're rolling, next:**
2. Write what "done" would mean for that one defect, in one sentence. · 3. If it's under an hour's work, schedule the hour. If it's bigger, the real task was "decide scope" and you've now started it.

## Tips for best results

- Name the specific task, not the project; the skill shrinks one thing at a time.
- If the tiny step still feels heavy, say so; it shrinks again (the bar can always go lower).
- Take the permission-to-stop seriously; removing the obligation is what removes the resistance.
