# Example: personal-statement

## What this skill does

Helps you write a personal statement that is specific, coherent and yours: a read of what this programme or role actually values, a through-line connecting your path to it, evidence that shows rather than asserts, and an authentic voice, built from your real experience.

## How to trigger it

### Example 1: a statement of purpose from scattered material

**You say:**
> "Help with my statement of purpose: MSc in Public Health, 600-word limit. My background: biology degree, 2 years as a pharmacy technician, volunteered at a vaccination clinic during a measles outbreak, grades good not stellar. I don't know what my 'story' is."

**Claude produces (excerpt):**

### Personal statement: MSc Public Health · 600 words

**What they want:** MPH programmes prize demonstrated commitment to population-level thinking and evidence you understand what the work actually is; research potential matters less than for a PhD.

**Your through-line:** you have watched individual-level healthcare fail at the population level, twice, from the inside. The pharmacy counter showed you adherence problems no prescription fixes; the outbreak clinic showed you what happens when prevention fails. Your path is not "I've always loved science"; it is "I keep meeting the limits of one-patient-at-a-time, and I want the tools to work at the level where these problems are actually solved." That is a story only you can tell.

**Structure**
- Opening: a specific moment from the outbreak clinic; one patient or one shift, concrete. Not "Public health has always fascinated me."
- Body: pharmacy counter evidence (the adherence patterns you noticed, what you did about them), then the clinic, then the course modules this trajectory points at; name two, specifically.
- Close: where this goes; the role you can picture, stated plainly.

**Show, don't assert:** "I'm passionate about prevention" becomes the detail of you noticing the same patients returning with preventable complications, and what that repetition taught you.

**Voice:** your draft phrase "leverage synergies between clinical and population paradigms" is not how you talk; "I kept seeing the same problem from two sides" is. Keep the second.

**On the grades:** one sentence of context if the programme asks; otherwise let the two years of evidence speak. Do not spend your 600 words apologising.

### Example 2: making an existing draft stronger

**You say:**
> "Make my statement stronger [pasted draft: opens with a Gandhi quote, lists 6 achievements in order, says 'passionate' 3 times]."

**Claude produces (excerpt):**

**The three structural problems, in order of damage:**
1. **No through-line:** six achievements in sequence is a CV in prose; a reader finishes knowing what you did and not why it points here. Your materials suggest the thread is [X]; everything that doesn't serve it gets cut, which is achievements 2 and 5.
2. **The borrowed opening:** admissions readers see the Gandhi quote weekly. Your own paragraph 4 contains your real opening: the sentence about [specific moment]. Move it to the top.
3. **Asserted passion:** all three "passionate"s deleted; each is replaced by the evidence sentence already sitting nearby in your draft. The proof was there; the assertion was burying it.

## Tips for best results

- Give the real material, including what feels unimpressive; through-lines hide in the ordinary parts.
- Name the programme and word limit; what they value decides what to emphasise.
- Expect drawing-out, not invention; nothing goes in the statement that didn't happen.
