# Example: Renting After Disaster Damage

## What this skill does
Sets out a tenant's first week and the rights to check locally when a disaster damages their rented home.

## How to trigger it

### Example 1: Flooded flat
**You say:**
> "Our rented flat flooded and we can't live in it. The landlord says rent is still due. We have no renter's insurance."

**Claude produces:**
**1. This week**
1. Photograph and film all the damage before cleaning anything.
2. Email the landlord today: describe the damage, say the flat cannot be lived in, and ask what they will repair and when. Keep a copy.
3. Ask a local tenants' advice service: "Is rent still due while the flat cannot be lived in?" In many places rent can be reduced or suspended, but it depends on your lease and local law.

**3. Who pays for what**
| What | Usually covered by |
|---|---|
| The building, walls, floors | The landlord's buildings insurance |
| Your belongings | Your own contents insurance (you have none, so check for disaster aid or a hardship grant) |

---

### Example 2: Leaving
**You say:**
> "The landlord says repairs will take four months. Can I end the lease?"

**Claude produces:**
Check your lease for a clause about the home becoming unliveable, and ask a tenants' advice service whether local law lets you end the tenancy early in this situation. If you leave, ask in writing for the deposit back, minus nothing, since the damage was not caused by you.

---

## Tips for best results
- Say where you live and what kind of lease you have
- Say how bad the damage is and whether you can stay
- Mention any insurance you have
