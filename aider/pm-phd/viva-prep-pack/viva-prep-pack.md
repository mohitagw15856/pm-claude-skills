# Viva Prep Pack

Candidates over-prepare by rereading the thesis and under-prepare by never saying their contribution aloud. This skill builds the document to study from: what the thesis claims, where an examiner will push, and how to answer honestly when the push lands on a real weakness. It prepares; it does not predict the outcome or script answers to memorise.

## What This Skill Produces

- A three-minute spoken summary and a one-sentence contribution
- Predicted questions grouped as opening, per chapter, big picture and closing, each with an answer outline
- A weak-points register: the limitation, why the choice was made, what the candidate would do differently
- A crib sheet per chapter: claim, key evidence, key sources, page numbers to find fast
- Examiner notes: likely interests based on their published work, which the candidate supplies
- A two-week plan and a day-before checklist

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:

- Field, country or institution (viva formats differ: closed oral examination, public defence, committee)
- Thesis title, abstract, research questions and chapter list
- The contribution as the candidate sees it
- Methods and the decisions the candidate is least sure about
- Known limitations and anything a supervisor has flagged
- Examiners' names and research areas, if known
- Date of the viva and time available to prepare

## Framework

1. **Contribution in one sentence.** "This thesis shows X, which matters because Y, and it is new because Z."
2. **Three-minute summary.** Problem, gap, approach, main findings, contribution. Written to be spoken.
3. **Questions examiners reliably ask.** Why this topic; what is original; why this method and not another; how do you know the findings are sound; what would you change; how does it relate to a named body of work; what next; what should be published.
4. **Per-chapter questions.** Two to four for each chapter, aimed at its claim and its weakest step.
5. **Own the limits.** For each weak point: name it before the examiner does, give the reasoning at the time, the effect on the conclusions, and the improvement.
6. **Answer shape.** Pause, answer the question asked in a sentence, support it, stop. It is acceptable to ask for the question again or to say "I had not considered that; my first thought is...".
7. **Examiner lens.** An examiner tends to ask from their own area. Map their interests to the chapters they touch.
8. **Know the document.** Tabs or a page index for key figures, definitions and the limitations section.

## Quality Checks

- [ ] The contribution fits in one sentence and names what is new
- [ ] Every chapter has questions aimed at its weakest step
- [ ] Each weak point has the reasoning and the effect on conclusions
- [ ] Answer outlines are bullets, not scripts
- [ ] The plan includes speaking answers aloud and a mock defence

## Anti-Patterns

- **Do not write answers to be memorised word for word.**
- **Do not hide limitations.** Examiners find them.
- **Do not predict the result or the corrections.**
- **Do not invent claims about the examiners' views.** Use only what the candidate supplies.
- **Do not prepare by rereading alone.** Practise aloud.

## Example Trigger Phrases

- "My viva is in three weeks. Build me a preparation pack from my abstract and chapter list."
- "What will examiners ask about a small-sample qualitative study?"
- "Help me say my contribution in one sentence."
- "My external examiner works on a rival theory. Where will they push?"
