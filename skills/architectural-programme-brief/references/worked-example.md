# Worked Example — Architectural Programme Brief

## The brief

A community trust has raised £1.4m and secured a 0.28 ha former depot site to replace a condemned village hall. The chair's brief is "a hall like the old one but bigger, plus a café, a nursery room, and offices we can rent out to cover running costs". The site has a 3 m level change, a public right of way along the northern boundary, and two mature oaks with preservation orders. Nearest residential property is 12 m from the southern boundary. They want to open in 18 months for the village's centenary. Construction rates locally are running £2,600–3,100/m² for this building type.

## The output

### Project brief: Village Hall Replacement · Ashcombe Community Trust · 2026-08-25 · rev A

**Project driver:** The existing hall is closed on structural grounds and the village has lost its only indoor gathering space. The building must host the events the village already runs — a 120-person function, weekly clubs, a pre-school session — and generate enough rental income to be self-sustaining, which the old hall never was.

**Adjacencies:** kitchen must adjoin hall **and** café · nursery must adjoin secure outdoor space and must **not** open off the hall (safeguarding and noise) · offices must have out-of-hours entry independent of the hall · deliveries must not cross the nursery outdoor area.

**Site & statutory:** 0.28 ha former depot · 3 m level change north–south · **public right of way along the northern boundary** (diversion is a separate legal process with its own timescale) · **two TPO oaks** — root protection areas remove usable ground · nearest residence 12 m south · previous depot use raises a contamination question that has not been investigated.

**Performance:** accessible throughout, step-free to every space · acoustic separation hall/nursery and hall/southern boundary (12 m to a dwelling, with amplified evening use) · daylight to nursery and offices · target: low running cost, since self-sustaining operation is the whole business case.

**Budget check:** 653 m² × £2,850/m² = **£1,861,000** against a stated budget of **£1,400,000** → **gap of approximately £461,000**, before fees, external works, contamination remediation, or fit-out.

**If there is a gap:** the options are to reduce area (the offices at 60 m² are the only element not serving the core purpose — but they are also the income that makes the building self-sustaining, so cutting them solves the capital problem and creates a revenue one); reduce specification; phase the offices as a later addition with the slab and services provided now; or raise a further £460k. **This is the decision the trust must take before design begins.**

**Programme:** 18 months to the centenary is not achievable on this site. Right-of-way diversion, a contamination investigation, planning determination, and tender each carry their own period before construction starts. A realistic first date is 26–30 months; the centenary should be planned in a marquee.

**Explicitly excluded:** car park resurfacing · external landscaping beyond the building's immediate curtilage · kitchen equipment beyond fixed items · IT and AV fit-out · the right-of-way diversion legal costs. Agreed with the trust on 2026-08-25.

**Assumptions:** no contamination requiring remediation (untested — if wrong, add £80–200k); TPO oaks retained; mains capacity adequate.

## What this example demonstrates

- **Activities, not rooms.** "180 standing / badminton" sets the clear height; "a hall like the old one but bigger" does not.
- **The budget is tested before anyone draws.** A £461k gap found in the brief is a conversation; found at tender it is a cancelled project.
- **The programme is contradicted with reasons.** Telling a client their date is impossible is the brief's job, and it is far cheaper now.
- **The genuine dilemma is stated rather than resolved.** Cutting the offices fixes capital cost and breaks the operating model — that is the trust's decision to make, and the brief's job is to make it visible.
- **Untested assumptions carry a price.** The contamination line converts an unknown into a number the client can weigh.
