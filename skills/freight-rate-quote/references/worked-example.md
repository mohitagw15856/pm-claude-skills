# Worked Example — Freight Rate Quote

## The brief

A 12-truck dry-van carrier is asked to quote a weekly round from Manchester to Aberdeen: 330 loaded miles, general palletised freight, 24 tonnes. The customer offers £1,150 per load and pays on 60 days. The nearest reload out of Aberdeen typically takes a day and a half to find, and pays thinly. The dispatcher's instinct is that £1,150 "sounds about right" because it works out over £3 a mile.

Fleet costs: vehicle payments £1,450/month · insurance £520/month · licensing and permits £180/month · overhead allocation £900/month per truck. Fuel £0.62/mile · tyres £0.05/mile · maintenance £0.14/mile · driver £0.58/mile. Realistic annual mileage per truck: 78,000.

**Rates:** break-even £771.90 · target at 18% margin £941 · **offered £1,150** · per loaded mile £3.48 · **revenue per day £460**

**Recommendation: Accept with conditions.**

The per-loaded-mile figure of £3.48 is the number that made this look comfortable, and it is the wrong number. Against **total** miles the rate is £2.77/mile, still above the £1.86 cost. The margin is real — £378 per load, 33% — but it is earned over **2.5 days**, not one, because of the Aberdeen reload wait. Revenue per day of £460 is the honest comparison against other lanes.

Conditions: detention terms as above written into the contract with the evidence requirement, since a 2-hour delay at either end removes a fifth of the margin; and 60-day payment priced in — at 12 loads a month this lane carries roughly £13,800 of working capital before the first invoice clears.

**Sensitivity:** fuel +15% → margin falls to £340 (−10%) · 4h unpaid detention → £238 (−37%) · reload takes +1 day → revenue/day falls to £328, below two of our existing lanes.

## What this example demonstrates

- **Per-loaded-mile is the trap.** £3.48 looks excellent; £2.77 per total mile is the truth, and revenue-per-day is what actually ranks the lane.
- **Deadhead at both ends is costed**, including the 60 miles chasing a reload out of a weak market.
- **Time is priced, not just distance.** The 1.5-day reload wait is the single biggest fact about this lane and does not appear anywhere in the rate itself.
- **Detention is made collectable.** The rate is worth nothing without the evidence clause; the sensitivity line shows why.
- **Payment terms are surfaced as working capital**, which is what actually kills small carriers on otherwise profitable lanes.
