# Personal Statement

A personal statement fails when it's a résumé rewritten in paragraphs, or a generic "I've always been passionate" essay that could be about anyone. A strong one has a through-line — why you, why this, and the evidence — told in your own voice. This helps you find that thread and build the statement from your genuine experience, tuned to what the specific program or role is looking for.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A fit read** — what this specific program/role values and wants to see (motivation, fit, potential), so the statement targets it
- **A clear through-line** — the central thread (your motivation and why you fit) that gives the statement coherence instead of a list
- **A show-don't-list structure** — an opening, evidence from real experience that demonstrates your claims, and a close on fit and goals
- **Evidence over assertion** — specific experiences and results that prove your qualities, not adjectives about yourself
- **An authentic voice** — genuinely yours, not inflated or generic
- **Drawn from you** — prompts to surface your material; it shapes your truth, it doesn't fabricate

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The target** — the program/role, institution/company, and any prompt or word limit
- **What they want** — the field, and what this program/role emphasizes
- **Your material** — experiences, motivations, results, and goals (as much as you'll share)
- **Your angle** — why you want this and what makes you a fit
- **Any draft** — existing text to strengthen

## Framework: Through-Line, Evidence, Fit

1. **Understand what they want.** Different programs/roles prize different things (research potential, clinical experience, specific skills, motivation) — target the statement to this, not a one-size essay.
2. **Find the through-line.** A single coherent thread — your motivation and the logic of your path to *this* — turns a list of achievements into a story with direction.
3. **Show with evidence.** Demonstrate your qualities through specific experiences and results, rather than asserting "I'm dedicated and hardworking."
4. **Structure with purpose.** Hook the reader, build the case with real material, and close on why you fit and where you're headed.
5. **Sound like you.** Keep an authentic, genuine voice; avoid inflation and cliché, which admissions readers see instantly.
6. **Stay honest and on-brief.** Build from real experience within the word limit and prompt — never invent.

## Output Format

### Personal statement: [program/role] · [field] · limit [x]

**What they want:** [program/role values → what to emphasize].
**Your through-line:** [the central motivation/fit thread].
**Structure**
- Opening: [a hook rooted in something real].
- Body: [evidence — experiences/results that show your qualities and path].
- Close: [why you fit + your goals].

**Show, don't assert:** [replace "I'm dedicated" with proof].
**Voice:** [authentic, not inflated].

> Built from *your* real experience — this helps you tell your story, not invent one.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Targets what the specific program/role values
- [ ] Has a clear motivation/fit through-line, not a list
- [ ] Shows qualities with evidence rather than asserting them
- [ ] Uses a purposeful hook-build-close structure
- [ ] Keeps an authentic voice, no cliché/inflation
- [ ] Stays within the prompt/word limit; nothing fabricated

## Anti-Patterns
- **A résumé in prose** — listing achievements with no thread.
- **Generic "always been passionate"** openings.
- **Asserting qualities** with no evidence.
- **Ignoring what the specific program/role wants.**
- **Inflated voice or fabricated** experiences.
