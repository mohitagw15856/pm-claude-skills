# Jury Duty Guide

A jury summons is a legal obligation, not junk mail — but people either panic about it or ignore it, both of which cause problems. This explains what your summons actually requires, the legitimate ways to defer or be excused if you genuinely can't serve, and what the day itself involves — so you handle it correctly and without unnecessary stress.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A summons read** — what it's asking, the response deadline, and the obligation (responding is mandatory even if you seek to be excused)
- **Deferral/excusal options** — the legitimate grounds (hardship, timing conflict, eligibility, prior service) and how to request them properly
- **What to expect** — the selection process, how long service may last, and what happens each day
- **Practical prep** — notifying your employer, pay/protection rules, childcare/logistics, what to bring
- **A consequences warning** — ignoring a summons can mean fines or worse; respond even to ask for a change
- **A jurisdiction flag** — rules, pay, and processes vary by location; not legal advice

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The summons** — what it says, the date, and the response deadline
- **Your situation** — any genuine conflict (work, health, caregiving, travel, eligibility)
- **Your goal** — serve as scheduled, defer to a better date, or seek excusal
- **Work context** — employer, and whether you're worried about pay/time off
- **Location** — determines the rules, pay, and process

## Framework: Respond, Request If Needed, Prepare

1. **Respond on time — always.** Whatever your goal, you must respond to the summons by the deadline; non-response is the actual risk, not the service itself.
2. **Know the legitimate options.** Deferral (to a later date) and excusal/hardship (medical, caregiving, essential-work, financial, or ineligibility) exist — but must be requested properly with any required proof.
3. **Ask honestly, not to "dodge."** Frame a genuine conflict clearly; don't fabricate — and understand many people can and do serve without hardship.
4. **Prepare practically.** Tell your employer (there are usually job protections), check pay rules, arrange logistics, and know what to bring and expect.
5. **Understand the day.** Selection may or may not place you on a jury; service length varies. Knowing the flow removes most of the anxiety.
6. **Heed the consequences.** Ignoring a summons can bring penalties — respond even if only to request a change.

## Output Format

### Jury duty: summoned [date] · goal: [serve/defer/excuse]

**Your obligation:** respond by [deadline] — mandatory even to request a change.
**Options:** defer to [later date] · or excusal/hardship on grounds of [medical/caregiving/work/financial/ineligible] — request via [method], with [any proof].
**On the day:** [selection process · likely length · what happens].
**Prepare:** notify employer (job protections) · check pay/allowance · logistics · what to bring.

> Not legal advice. Rules, pay, and excusal grounds vary by jurisdiction — follow the instructions on your summons and your local court's guidance.

## Anti-Patterns
- **Coaching how to "dodge"** duty or fabricate an excuse.
- **Ignoring the summons** — the one genuinely risky move.
- **Treating excusal as automatic** rather than a proper request.
- **Missing employer-notice/pay** practicalities.
- **Asserting one process** across jurisdictions.
