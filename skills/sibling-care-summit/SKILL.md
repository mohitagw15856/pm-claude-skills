# Sibling Care Summit Skill

Parent-care conflict between siblings is rarely about the parent — it's
about 1994. The sister who lives nearby slides into everything-by-default;
the brother abroad sends money and gets called uninvolved; the family
group chat holds three parallel arguments and zero decisions. The summit is
the alternative: one structured conversation held *before* the crisis (or
early in it), where the work is inventoried honestly, divided fairly —
fair meaning proportional to real capacity, never split-equally-on-paper —
and written down, because unwritten care agreements dissolve into the
default sibling doing everything and resenting everyone.

## What This Skill Produces

- A **summit agenda** (90 minutes, video counts): parents' current
  reality → what they want → the work inventory → the division → decision
  rules → the memo
- The **care-share worksheet**: every recurring task with its real weekly
  cost in hours/money/emotional load, and the three currencies siblings
  can pay in — time, money, and coordination — counted as equally real
- **Decision rules** written while everyone's calm: what needs all
  siblings, what the on-the-ground sibling just decides, what the parents
  still own (most things, longer than the family assumes)
- The **family memo**: one page, who does what, revisit date — the
  document that replaces the group-chat archaeology
- **De-escalation moves** for the two classic derailments: the ledger of
  1994, and "you're not here, you don't get a vote"

## Required Inputs

Ask for (if not already provided):
- The cast: siblings (and partners-who-do-care-work), where each lives,
  real constraints (jobs, kids, money, health) — and the honest current
  split of who does what
- The parents: current needs (or the approaching ones), what they've said
  they want, how much they know about this meeting happening — *the
  parents are participants in their own lives; the summit plans support,
  it doesn't dispose of them*
- The friction, honestly: old roles, money asymmetries, the sibling who
  won't engage
- What's triggering this now

## Framework

1. **Set the frame before the facts.** The opener that works: "we're here
   so mum gets what she wants and none of us burns out or gets resented —
   not to relitigate anything." Old-roles regression is named as a risk
   *in the agenda* — naming it out loud is 80% of preventing it.
2. **Start from the parents' wants, not the siblings' worries.** What have
   the parents actually said? What must be asked rather than assumed
   ([[aging-parent-talks]] does that conversation)? Decisions the parents
   can still make stay theirs — the summit's scope is the support around
   those decisions.
3. **Inventory the invisible work.** Every recurring task on the
   worksheet with true weekly cost — including the invisible ones:
   coordination, being the on-call name, the mental load of noticing.
   The nearby sibling's list is always longer than anyone knew; putting
   it on paper is the moment the summit usually turns.
4. **Divide by capacity across three currencies.** Time (visits, errands,
   appointments) · money (the abroad sibling's real contribution —
   named as care, not as guilt payment) · coordination (running the
   rota, the paperwork, the calls). Fair = proportional to capacity and
   *agreed as fair by the person carrying each load*. Equal-on-paper is
   how resentment gets notarized.
5. **Write decision rules for three tiers.** Parents decide (default,
   most things) · on-the-ground sibling decides alone (day-to-day, under
   a named threshold) · all siblings decide (money above X, care-setting
   changes, anything irreversible) — with the tie-break agreed now
   (parent's stated preference wins; failing that, the named lead for
   that domain). Verify-local flag on anything touching legal authority
   (powers of attorney etc. — look up, don't assume).
6. **Memo and revisit, or it didn't happen.** One page: the split, the
   rules, the money handling (shared care account with visibility beats
   opaque reimbursement), the revisit date (90 days, or at any change in
   the parents' needs). The de-escalation lines stay in the pack:
   "1994 is real and it's not tonight's agenda" · "distance changes your
   currency, not your membership."


## Quality Checks

- [ ] The parents' own wants open the agenda, and their retained decisions
      are listed explicitly
- [ ] The worksheet includes coordination and mental load as real tasks
- [ ] Money contributions are framed as care, never as buyout or guilt
- [ ] Decision tiers have actual thresholds, and legal-authority items
      carry verify-local flags
- [ ] The memo fits one page and has a revisit date — no memo, no summit

## Anti-Patterns

- [ ] Do not build an equal split — build a fair one, and let the people
      carrying each load certify the fairness
- [ ] Do not let the summit decide things that are still the parents' to
      decide
- [ ] Do not encode the ledger of 1994 anywhere — lessons about capacity
      yes, scorekeeping no
- [ ] Do not assert POA/guardianship/benefits rules — flag and route to
      local advice
- [ ] Do not paper over a non-engaging sibling with optimistic rows —
      plan the real split with who's actually at the table, and leave
      their column open, not filled in absentia
