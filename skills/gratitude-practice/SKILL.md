# Gratitude Practice

Gratitude practices work — but most fizzle because they're vague ("list three things") and quickly become rote, or they curdle into pretending everything's fine. This sets up a specific, sustainable practice: concrete prompts, enough variety to stay fresh, and a grounded stance that holds space for hard things instead of papering over them.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A concrete format** — how many items, how specific, and *why* (specificity is what makes it land)
- **An anchor & cadence** — when and how often (daily isn't always best), tied to an existing routine
- **Variety** — rotating angles (people, small moments, challenges overcome) so it doesn't go stale
- **A depth option** — occasionally going deeper on one thing vs. listing several
- **An honest framing** — gratitude alongside difficulty, not toxic positivity that denies real problems

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **Your goal** — general wellbeing, a reset during a hard time, or building the habit
- **Experience** — tried it before (and what fizzled) or brand new
- **Time & cadence** — daily, a few times a week, how many minutes
- **Format preference** — written, spoken, shared with someone, app
- **Context** — anything you want it to help with (stress, perspective, relationships)

## Framework: Specific, Varied, Grounded

1. **Be specific, not generic.** "My friend texted to check on me today" beats "my friends" — detail is what makes gratitude actually shift mood.
2. **Right-size the cadence.** Daily can dull the effect for some; a few intentional times a week often lands harder. Fit it to the person.
3. **Rotate the lens.** People, small sensory moments, things that went right, hard things you got through — variety keeps it from becoming a checkbox.
4. **Anchor it.** Attach to an existing routine (coffee, bedtime) so it happens without relying on memory.
5. **Stay honest.** Gratitude sits *next to* difficulty, not on top of it — never imply someone should just be grateful instead of feeling what's real.

## Output Format

### Gratitude practice: [goal] · [cadence] · [format]

**Format:** [# items, how specific, written/spoken].
**Anchor:** after [existing routine], at [time].
**Cadence:** [daily / few times a week — why].

**Rotate through**
- People · small moments · things that went right · hard things you moved through.

**Go-deeper option:** once a week, one thing in detail — why it mattered.

> Gratitude complements hard feelings; it doesn't replace them. If things are genuinely heavy, this isn't about forcing positivity.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Emphasizes specificity over generic lists
- [ ] Cadence is right-sized, not blindly "daily"
- [ ] Includes variety so it doesn't go stale
- [ ] Anchored to an existing routine
- [ ] Explicitly avoids toxic positivity / denying real difficulty

## Anti-Patterns
- **Vague "list three things"** with no specificity guidance.
- **Rote repetition** — same items daily until it's meaningless.
- **Toxic positivity** — implying gratitude should erase hard feelings.
- **No anchor** — relying on remembering.
- **One rigid format** that gets stale fast.

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "Help me start a gratitude practice that I'll actually keep up."
- "I tried a gratitude journal and it got boring — how do I fix that?"
- "A gratitude routine to help during a stressful period."
- "How specific should my gratitude list be?"
- "Set up a simple gratitude habit anchored to my morning coffee."
