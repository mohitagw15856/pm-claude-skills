# Scholarship Essay

Scholarship committees read hundreds of essays that all say "education is important to me" and "I want to help people." Yours stands out by being specific and true: a real story, a genuine angle, and a clear tie to who you are and what the scholarship is for. This helps you find that angle and shape it — drawing the essay out of *your* experience, never inventing one.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A prompt decode** — what the question is actually asking and what the committee is looking for (fit with the scholarship's values, not just need)
- **Your angle** — a specific, genuine story or theme from your real experience that answers the prompt distinctively
- **A structure** — an opening hook, a story/argument that builds, and a close that ties to the future and the award
- **Specifics over platitudes** — concrete details and moments instead of the generic sentiments everyone writes
- **Your authentic voice** — phrasing that sounds like you, not a thesaurus
- **Drawn from you** — prompts to surface your real material; it helps you write, it doesn't fabricate experiences

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The prompt** — the exact essay question and any word limit
- **The scholarship** — who gives it and what they value (mission, criteria)
- **Your story** — relevant experiences, challenges, goals, and what matters to you (as much as you'll share)
- **Your goal** — the field/path and how the scholarship fits it
- **Any drafts** — what you've written so far

## Framework: Specific, True, On-Prompt

1. **Decode the prompt and the giver.** Understand what's being asked *and* what the scholarship values — align your essay with both, not a generic "why I deserve money."
2. **Find a specific, genuine angle.** Draw a real story or theme from the person's experience that's distinctive; specificity is what separates a memorable essay from the pile.
3. **Hook, build, land.** Open with something that pulls the reader in, develop the story/argument with real detail, and close by connecting to the future and the award's purpose.
4. **Show, don't platitude.** Concrete moments and details beat "I've always been passionate about..." Cut the clichés.
5. **Keep it authentically theirs.** Preserve the applicant's own voice and truth — an essay that sounds AI-written or fabricated backfires.
6. **Answer the actual question.** However good the story, it must clearly address the prompt within the word limit.

## Output Format

### Scholarship essay: [prompt] · [scholarship values] · limit [x words]

**What they're looking for:** [prompt intent + scholarship's values/fit].
**Your angle:** [the specific, genuine story/theme to build on].
**Structure**
- Hook: [an opening that pulls in].
- Body: [the story/argument with real specifics].
- Close: [tie to your future + why this scholarship].

**Make it specific:** [replace platitudes with concrete moments].
**Your voice:** [keep it authentically yours].

> Written from *your* real experiences — this helps you tell your story, it won't invent one.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Decodes the prompt and the scholarship's values
- [ ] Builds on a specific, genuine angle from the applicant's story
- [ ] Has a hook-build-close structure
- [ ] Replaces platitudes with concrete specifics
- [ ] Preserves the applicant's authentic voice
- [ ] Answers the prompt within the word limit; nothing fabricated

## Anti-Patterns
- **Generic sentiments** ("education matters," "I want to help people") everyone writes.
- **Ignoring the scholarship's specific values.**
- **A great story that doesn't answer the prompt.**
- **Fabricated experiences or hardship.**
- **A thesaurus-y voice** that isn't the applicant's.
