# Kids' Online-Safety Plan

Locking everything down breeds workarounds and secrecy; doing nothing leaves kids exposed. Good online safety is settings *plus* trust: age-appropriate controls, a clear family agreement, and — the part that actually protects — an open line so a kid will come to you when something goes wrong. This builds that, tuned to the child's age.

## What This Skill Produces

- **Age-tuned settings** — device, app, and platform controls appropriate to the child's age and maturity
- **A family agreement** — shared, age-appropriate rules on time, content, sharing, and strangers, that the kid helps shape
- **The conversations** — the ongoing talks (about strangers, sharing, pressure, mistakes) that matter more than any filter
- **Warning signs** — behavioral changes that may signal bullying, grooming, or distress
- **A response plan** — what to do (calmly) if something goes wrong, and where to get help/report

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The child's age(s)** — the single biggest factor
- **Devices & platforms** — what they use (games, social, messaging, tablets, phones)
- **Current setup** — any controls or rules already in place
- **Your concern** — general safety, a specific worry, a recent incident
- **Your parenting balance** — how you weigh protection vs independence/trust

## Framework: Settings + Trust, By Age

1. **Match to age and maturity.** A 7-year-old and a 15-year-old need very different plans — controls loosen and conversations deepen as they grow.
2. **Set the technical baseline.** Age-appropriate content filters, privacy settings, screen-time tools, and safe defaults on the platforms they actually use.
3. **Agree, don't just impose.** A family agreement the child helps write gets buy-in; pure surveillance invites secret accounts and lies.
4. **Prioritize the open door.** The best protection is a kid who'll tell you when something's wrong — react to mistakes with calm, not punishment, so they keep coming to you.
5. **Watch, and know how to respond.** Learn the warning signs and have a plan for bullying, contact from strangers, or exposure — including how to report and where to get help.

## Output Format

### Online-safety plan: child age [x] · [platforms] · concern: [y]

**Settings (age-appropriate):** [device/app/platform controls + privacy defaults].
**Family agreement:** [time · content · sharing · stranger rules — co-created].
**Talk about (ongoing):** strangers/contact · what's safe to share · pressure & mistakes · coming to you.
**Warning signs:** [behavioral changes to watch].
**If something goes wrong:** [calm response · how to report/block · where to get help].

**Balance:** [tighter now / more independence as they show maturity].

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Plan is tuned to the child's specific age/maturity
- [ ] Covers technical settings on the platforms they actually use
- [ ] Includes a co-created family agreement, not just top-down bans
- [ ] Emphasizes ongoing conversation and an open, non-punitive door
- [ ] Lists warning signs and a calm response/reporting plan
- [ ] Balances protection with age-appropriate independence

## Anti-Patterns
- **Pure surveillance/lockdown** that breeds secret accounts.
- **One-size rules** ignoring the child's age.
- **Set-and-forget controls** with no conversation.
- **Punishing mistakes harshly** so the kid hides the next problem.
- **No plan** for when something actually goes wrong.

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "How do I keep my 9-year-old safe online?"
- "Set up parental controls and rules for my kids' tablets."
- "My teenager's on social media — what should our agreement be?"
- "What are the warning signs my child is being bullied or groomed online?"
- "Screen-time and online rules that won't start a war with my kid."
