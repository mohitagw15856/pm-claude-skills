# Worked Example — Medication Therapy Review

## The brief

A 78-year-old is referred for review after a fall. Record lists eleven medicines from three prescribers (GP, cardiology, and a pain clinic). eGFR 38 mL/min/1.73m², recorded four months ago. Discharged from hospital six weeks ago after the fall; the discharge summary added one medicine and the GP list still shows one that was stopped on the ward. On questioning, the patient says they halve one tablet "because it made me dizzy", stopped another three months ago without telling anyone, and buy something over the counter for sleep. They say what they want is "to stop rattling" and to be able to garden again.

## The output

### Medication review: [patient] · 2026-08-25 · [pharmacist]

**Reconciliation:** 11 on record · 9 actually taking · **discrepancies:**
- One stopped on the ward six weeks ago, still on the GP repeat — patient has continued taking it
- One self-discontinued three months ago (patient reports it "did nothing"), still issued monthly
- One self-halved for dizziness — taking 50% of the prescribed dose
- One OTC sedating antihistamine for sleep, not on any list

**Drug therapy problems**

| # | Problem | Type | Medicines involved | Potential harm | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Duplicate therapy from unreconciled discharge | Therapy without indication | Ward-stopped agent still on repeat | **High** — additive effect with the cardiology agent, and a plausible contributor to the fall | Confirm against the discharge summary and remove from the repeat |
| 2 | Sedating OTC antihistamine alongside two centrally acting medicines | Adverse reaction risk | OTC + two prescribed | **High** — additive sedation and falls risk in a 78-year-old | Discuss a non-sedating alternative for sleep with the patient and GP |
| 3 | Dose not adjusted for current renal function | Dose too high | Renally cleared agent | **High** — eGFR 38 with a dose set when function was better; last U&Es four months old | Request repeat U&Es and review dose against current function |
| 4 | Self-halved dose after dizziness | Adherence / adverse reaction | Antihypertensive | Moderate — BP control unknown at the actual dose taken | Record the true dose taken; propose the prescriber review at the dose actually being taken, not the prescribed one |
| 5 | Self-discontinued three months ago, still dispensed | Therapy without indication | Statin | Low harm, real waste and record inaccuracy | Confirm intention with the patient, then stop the issue |
| 6 | Eleven items, twice-daily burden | Adherence | Whole regimen | Moderate | After 1–3 resolve, review whether the count can fall — this is the patient's own stated goal |

**Patient goals:** "stop rattling" (fewer items) and return to gardening (mobility, no dizziness).
**Conflicts with current regimen:** the sedating OTC and the possibly over-dosed renal agent both work directly against the gardening goal — these are the recommendations most likely to be accepted, and should lead.

**Recommendations to prescriber** — highest harm first

1. **Duplicate therapy continuing post-discharge.** Rationale: the ward stopped it six weeks ago; the repeat was never updated and the patient has continued it alongside the cardiology agent. Proposed: verify against the discharge summary and remove from the repeat template.
2. **Sedating OTC antihistamine taken nightly** with two centrally acting prescribed medicines. Rationale: additive sedation, and the fall is recent. Proposed: agree a non-sedating approach to sleep.
3. **Renally cleared agent at a dose set before the current eGFR.** Rationale: eGFR 38, last checked four months ago. Proposed: repeat U&Es, then review the dose against current function.

**Monitoring & follow-up:** U&Es requested — GP, within 2 weeks · BP at the dose actually taken — practice nurse, 4 weeks · falls review — 6 weeks · pharmacist re-review once 1–3 are actioned.

**Discussed with patient:** explained why the OTC sleep aid matters after a fall; they agreed to stop it pending an alternative. Declined to stop the halved antihypertensive entirely — wants the dizziness discussed with the GP first, which is recorded and reasonable.

> A structured reasoning and documentation framework for a licensed pharmacist. It does not diagnose or determine appropriateness for an individual, and recommends no specific drug or dose.

## What this example demonstrates

- **Reconciliation before assessment.** Four of the six problems exist only in the gap between the record and what the patient actually takes — reviewing the list alone would have found two.
- **Every problem is classified**, which is what turns a list into something a prescriber can act on.
- **Prioritised by harm, not by ease.** The easiest fix (the unused statin) is fifth.
- **Patient goals are used to sequence, not decorate.** The recommendations that serve "gardening again" are the ones that will actually be accepted.
- **A declined recommendation is recorded as the patient's reasoned choice**, not as non-compliance.
