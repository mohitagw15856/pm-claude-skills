# Respite-Care Plan

"I can't leave them" is how caregivers burn out — but respite (planned breaks) is exactly what makes long-term caregiving sustainable. The barriers are real: guilt, not trusting anyone else, cost, and the logistics of handing off. This plans a genuine break: who can cover, the handoff so they're set up to succeed, how to get past the barriers, and how to actually rest instead of worrying the whole time — so the break happens and repeats. Not medical advice.

## What This Skill Produces

- **Coverage options** — the realistic ways to get covered for your situation: family/friends, paid in-home respite, adult day programs, or a short-stay facility break, with pros/cons
- **A handoff pack** — what whoever covers needs to succeed: the routine, medications, contacts, what-ifs, and the person's preferences — so you can actually step away
- **Barrier-busting** — addressing the specific things stopping you: guilt, not trusting others, cost/funding to look into, and the logistics
- **A "actually rest" plan** — how to use the break to genuinely recover (and quiet the worry) rather than spend it anxious or doing chores
- **A make-it-repeatable setup** — turning a one-off into a regular, sustainable rhythm of breaks
- **A boundary** — logistics support, not medical advice; complex care needs professional respite arrangements

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **Who you care for** — their needs and how much supervision/care they require
- **The break you need** — a few hours, a day, or longer
- **Who/what's available** — family, budget for paid help, local services
- **The barrier** — guilt, trust, cost, logistics, or "no one else can do it"
- **What rest looks like for you** — what would actually recharge you

## Framework: Arrange, Hand Off, Rest, Repeat

1. **Find the coverage.** Map the realistic options — a family member, paid respite worker, a day program, or a short-stay — matched to the care level and your budget.
2. **Prepare the handoff.** The trust barrier shrinks when whoever covers has a clear pack: routine, medications, contacts, warning signs, and preferences. Set them up to succeed.
3. **Bust the barriers.** Guilt (respite is necessary, not selfish), trust (a prepared, competent stand-in), cost (funding routes to research), and logistics (start small) — address each directly.
4. **Plan to actually rest.** A break spent worrying or catching up on chores isn't respite — decide in advance how you'll genuinely recover, and how to quiet the worry (a check-in call, trusting the pack).
5. **Make it repeatable.** One break isn't enough — set up a regular rhythm so respite is a sustained part of the caregiving, not a rare emergency.
6. **Get professional help for complex needs.** Where care is medically complex, use professional respite services; this handles the planning, not the medical care.

## Output Format

### Respite plan: caring for [who] · break needed [length]

**Coverage options:** [family/friends · paid in-home respite · day program · short-stay] — with pros/cons for you.
**Handoff pack (so you can step away):** routine · medications · contacts · warning signs · preferences.
**Bust the barriers:** guilt → [necessary, not selfish] · trust → [prepared stand-in] · cost → [funding to research] · logistics → [start small].
**Actually rest:** [how to genuinely recover + quiet the worry — a check-in, trusting the pack].
**Make it repeatable:** [a regular rhythm of breaks, not a one-off].

> Logistics support — not medical advice. For medically complex care, arrange professional respite services.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Maps realistic coverage options for the care level and budget
- [ ] Provides a handoff pack so stepping away is possible
- [ ] Addresses the specific barriers (guilt, trust, cost, logistics)
- [ ] Plans for genuine rest, not worry or chores
- [ ] Sets up a repeatable rhythm, not a one-off
- [ ] Flags professional respite for complex needs; not medical advice

## Anti-Patterns
- **"You deserve a break"** with no actual coverage plan.
- **No handoff pack** — so the caregiver can't trust leaving.
- **Ignoring the guilt/trust barriers.**
- **A break spent worrying** or doing chores.
- **A one-off** instead of a sustainable rhythm.

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "I desperately need a break from caring for my husband — how do I arrange it?"
- "How do I get respite care so I can rest?"
- "I can't leave my mom alone and I'm exhausted. Help me get time off."
- "I feel too guilty to take a break from caregiving."
- "Set up a regular break in my caregiving so I don't collapse."
