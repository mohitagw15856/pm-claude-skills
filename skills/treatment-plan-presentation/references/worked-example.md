# Worked Example — Dental Treatment Plan Presentation

## The brief

A 41-year-old patient attends about a cracked lower-left molar that "twinges on cold". Charting and radiographs find, beyond the chief complaint: a fractured cusp on #19 with a deep existing amalgam, active caries on #3 and #14, generalised 4–5 mm pockets with bleeding on probing in both posterior sextants, and worn incisal edges the patient has mentioned disliking in photographs. They have not attended in four years after a painful extraction elsewhere. Plan coverage is £1,200 annually with £950 remaining. They work shifts and can take limited time off. When the receptionist mentioned a treatment plan, they said "I just want the tooth that hurts sorted."

Fees: crown £850 · composite restoration £190 each · scaling and root planing £340 per quadrant · composite edge bonding £220 per tooth.

## The output

### Treatment plan: [patient] · 2026-08-25 · presented by Dr Okafor

**Chief concern in their words:** "It twinges when I drink anything cold, and I can feel a sharp bit with my tongue."

**What we found**

| Finding | Where | What it means | If untreated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fractured cusp over a deep filling | #19 (lower left molar) | The tooth flexes when you bite, which is the twinge; the crack runs under the old filling | Cracks travel. If it reaches the nerve this becomes root canal treatment or an extraction — typically months, not years |
| Active decay | #3, #14 | Two small cavities, not yet near the nerve | Each becomes a larger filling, then a crown, at roughly 4x the cost |
| Gum inflammation, 4–5 mm pockets | Both back sections | Bleeding gums and pockets deeper than a brush reaches | The bone that holds the teeth recedes; this is the usual reason teeth are lost after 40, and it is painless until late |
| Worn front edges | Upper front teeth | Enamel loss from grinding | Cosmetic today; more tooth is lost each year |

**Phase 1 — Urgent** · stop the crack progressing and treat active disease
- Treatment: crown #19, composite restorations #3 and #14 · Visits: 3 · Fee: £1,230 · Covered: £950 · **You pay: £280**

**Phase 2 — Functional** · stabilise the gum condition
- Treatment: scaling and root planing, two quadrants · Visits: 2 · Fee: £680 · Covered: £0 (annual maximum used) · **You pay: £680**

**Phase 3 — Elective** · the appearance they raised themselves
- Treatment: composite edge bonding, four upper incisors · Fee: £880 · **You pay: £880**

**If you do only Phase 1:** the pain stops and the crack is contained. The gum condition continues quietly — we would review in three months and the pockets will likely be deeper. This is a reasonable place to stop this year given the coverage limit, and it is what I would do in your position.

**Options discussed:**
- #19: crown (recommended) vs a large filling — the filling is £190 and buys perhaps a year, because it does not hold the cusp together; vs extraction, which costs less now and more later.
- Gum treatment: two quadrants now vs all four — splitting across the plan year was the patient's preference and is clinically acceptable here.
- No treatment: explained, including that the crack is the item with a clock on it.

**Decision today:** Phase 1 accepted; #19 booked for Thursday. Phase 2 deferred to January when coverage resets — patient's choice, recorded.
**Follow-up:** reception to call in December to book Phase 2 before the new plan year fills.

> Records the conversation, not the diagnosis. Fees, coverage and consequence horizons must be verified against the actual plan and chart before presenting.

## What this example demonstrates

- **It opens where the patient is**, not at the largest finding. The gum condition is arguably more serious long term, but leading with it would have lost them.
- **Consequences are specific and horizon-bounded** — "months, not years" for the crack, "painless until late" for the perio. No catastrophising, which the anti-patterns forbid.
- **The honest minimum is stated and endorsed.** Saying "this is what I would do in your position" is what converts a plan from a sales document into advice.
- **The declined phase is recorded as the patient's choice**, with a follow-up owner and date — the difference between a deferral and a loss.
