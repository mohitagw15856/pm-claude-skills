# Co-Parenting Messages

Co-parenting communication goes wrong when old relationship dynamics leak into logistics — a simple scheduling text becomes a fight. This drafts messages that stay businesslike and child-centered: brief, factual, and firm, with the emotional bait stripped out, so you address the actual issue (schedule, expense, decision) without reigniting conflict — and keep a clean record while you're at it.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A calm, child-centered draft** — the message written in a neutral, businesslike tone focused on the kids and the logistics
- **Bait removed** — provocations, blame, and digs (theirs and yours) filtered out so you don't escalate
- **A clear ask or answer** — one concrete request, decision, or response, not a rehash of grievances
- **Boundary phrasing** — how to hold a line (say no, decline a topic) firmly and politely
- **A documentation note** — keeping communication on the record (a co-parenting app or written channel) for clarity and, if needed, proceedings

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The topic** — schedule, pickup/dropoff, expenses, a parenting decision, or a sensitive issue
- **What was said** — their message, if you're replying (paste it)
- **Your goal** — arrange something, answer, hold a boundary, or de-escalate
- **The dynamic** — high-conflict, cooperative-ish, or somewhere between
- **Any constraints** — a custody agreement/order terms relevant to the ask

## Framework: Brief, Informative, Friendly, Firm

1. **Center the child, not the history.** Frame everything around the kids' needs and logistics; leave the relationship out of it.
2. **Strip the bait.** Remove blame, sarcasm, and reactions to *their* provocations — responding to bait is how logistics become fights. Answer only the substance.
3. **Keep it brief and factual (BIFF).** Brief, informative, friendly, and firm — enough to convey the point, not an essay that invites a rebuttal war.
4. **Make one clear ask/answer.** State the request, decision, or response plainly, with specifics (dates, amounts) — reduce back-and-forth.
5. **Hold boundaries calmly.** Decline off-topic or hostile threads politely and redirect to the child-focused matter.
6. **Keep the record.** Prefer written/app-based channels and a factual tone — useful for clarity now and if it ever matters legally.

## Output Format

### Co-parenting message: topic [x] · goal [y] · tone: [de-escalate]

**Draft**
> [Brief, factual, child-centered message: the specific ask/answer, dates/amounts as needed, warm-but-firm, no bait, no blame.]

**What I removed:** [the digs/reactions filtered out].
**Boundary line (if needed):** "[polite decline + redirect]".
**Keep on record:** [written/app channel; factual tone].

> Not legal advice. For custody/legal disputes, follow your agreement or order and consult a family-law professional.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Tone is neutral, businesslike, and child-centered
- [ ] Provocations/blame (theirs and yours) are stripped out
- [ ] Contains one clear ask or answer with specifics
- [ ] Holds any needed boundary politely and firmly
- [ ] Notes keeping communication on the record
- [ ] Avoids anything that escalates; flags not-legal-advice

## Anti-Patterns
- **Taking the bait** and firing back.
- **Rehashing the relationship** instead of the logistics.
- **An essay** that invites a point-by-point war.
- **Vague asks** that cause more back-and-forth.
- **Hostile "documentation"** written to score points rather than resolve.

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "Help me reply to my ex about swapping this weekend without it turning into a fight."
- "Write a calm message asking my co-parent to split a school expense."
- "My ex sent a nasty text about pickup — help me respond neutrally."
- "How do I tell my co-parent no to a schedule change, politely but firmly?"
- "Rewrite this message so it's child-focused and not combative."
