# Match Review

After a loss the temptation is to list every mistake; after a win, to learn nothing. This skill reviews performance against what the team was trying to do, keeps the result in proportion, and turns the review into next week's training. For youth teams it keeps the tone on learning and effort.

## What This Skill Produces

- A coach's review: plan, key moments, what worked, what to improve, with evidence
- A player-facing version of half a page that starts with what went well
- Private notes on individuals, phrased as development points
- Two or three training implications for the coming week
- One question to put to the players

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:

- Sport, age group, level, opposition and result
- The game plan and the season's current training emphasis
- The coach's notes by period or phase, and any stats or video observations
- Context: availability, conditions, travel, the standard of the opposition
- Who the review is for: coaching staff, players, parents, a club report
- Anything sensitive: an injury, a disciplinary matter, a player in a difficult patch

## Framework

1. **Judge against the plan, not the score.** Did the team do what it trained to do?
2. **Break the game into phases**: in possession, out of possession, transitions and set plays, or the equivalent for the sport.
3. **Evidence for each point.** A moment, a pattern or a number. "We lost the ball" becomes "we gave the ball away in our own half nine times, six under no pressure".
4. **Three and three.** Three things to keep, three to improve. More than that will not be remembered.
5. **Controllables.** Separate what the team controls from refereeing, luck and conditions. Mention the latter once at most.
6. **Individuals in private.** Development points go to the player one to one, framed as the next step. The team version names people only for praise.
7. **Link to training.** Each improvement point becomes a theme or a practice.
8. **Youth tone.** Effort, bravery and learning first. No public blame for a result.

## Output Format

### Match review: [team] v [opposition], [date], [result]

**The plan:** [what the team set out to do]

**By phase**
| Phase | What happened | Evidence |
|---|---|---|

**Keep**
1. [point and evidence]

**Improve**
1. [point, evidence, and the training response]

**Context, once:** [conditions, availability]

**Player-facing version**
> [What we did well. What we will work on. What is next.]

**Private individual notes (not for the group)**
| Player | Did well | Next step |
|---|---|---|

**Training this week:** [themes or practices]
**Question for the players:** [one open question]

**Assumptions and gaps**
- [What was assumed from limited notes]
- [What video or stats would confirm or change]

## Quality Checks

- [ ] The review is measured against the plan
- [ ] Every point has a moment, pattern or number behind it
- [ ] There are no more than three keep and three improve points
- [ ] No player is criticised by name in the team version
- [ ] Each improve point links to training
- [ ] Referee and luck get one mention at most

## Anti-Patterns

- **Do not write the review in the hour after the final whistle.**
- **Do not list every error.**
- **Do not let the scoreline write the review.** A poor win and a good loss both exist.
- **Do not share individual notes with the group or with parents.**
- **Do not invent statistics** to support an impression.

## Example Trigger Phrases

- "We lost 4-1 but played well for an hour. Help me review it honestly."
- "Turn these touchline notes into a review and a Monday team talk."
- "Write a short match report for the under-13s that focuses on what they did well."
- "What should we train this week based on these stats?"
