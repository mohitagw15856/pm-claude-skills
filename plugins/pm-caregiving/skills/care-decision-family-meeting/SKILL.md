# Care-Decision Family Meeting

Big care decisions — where a parent lives, what care they need, who pays, who does what — bring out every old family dynamic, and often end in a fight, a stalemate, or one exhausted person deciding alone and resenting it. A structured family meeting changes that. This gives you the agenda, preparation, ground rules, and facilitation to make it a shared, informed decision — including the person being cared for — with clear next steps. Not legal, medical, or financial advice.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A meeting agenda** — a clear structure (the situation, the options, everyone's input, working to a decision, next steps) so it doesn't spiral
- **Preparation** — the facts, options, and information to gather beforehand (a meeting without shared facts is just an argument), and getting everyone's views in advance
- **Ground rules** — norms to keep old sibling dynamics, blame, and one loud voice from derailing it (one person speaks at a time, focus on the parent's needs, no re-litigating history)
- **Including the person** — how to involve the one being cared for in the decision about their own life, to the extent they can
- **Disagreement navigation** — ways to work through the common conflicts (who does more, who pays, differing views on the parent's needs) toward an actual decision
- **Next steps with owners** — concrete actions, who does what, and when to revisit — so the meeting produces action, not just talk
- **A boundary** — this facilitates the decision; the medical, legal, and financial specifics need professionals

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The decision** — what needs deciding (living situation, care level, finances, division of duties)
- **Who's involved** — the family members, their locations, and the dynamics
- **The person being cared for** — their needs, wishes, and capacity to participate
- **The disagreements** — where views differ or tension exists
- **The facts available** — what's known vs. needs gathering (costs, options, medical input)

## Framework: Prepare, Structure, Decide Together

1. **Prepare so it's about facts.** Gather the options and information (care levels, costs, medical input, the parent's wishes) beforehand — decisions made without shared facts become fights about feelings.
2. **Get input in advance.** Ask everyone for their views before the meeting so quieter voices are heard and no one is blindsided.
3. **Set ground rules.** Explicit norms — one speaker at a time, focus on the parent's needs not old grievances, no blame, and a shared goal — keep family dynamics from hijacking it.
4. **Include the person.** As much as their capacity allows, the one being cared for should be part of deciding about their own life — not talked over.
5. **Work through disagreement.** Surface the real tensions (unequal load, money, differing assessments) and move toward a decision — sometimes a facilitator or professional helps if it's stuck.
6. **End with owners and next steps.** Concrete actions, who's responsible, and a date to revisit — so the emotional talk turns into a plan.
7. **Bring in professionals.** Medical, legal, and financial specifics need the right experts — the meeting decides direction, not those details.

## Output Format

### Family care meeting: deciding [what] · involved [who]

**Prepare (before):** gather [options · costs · medical input · parent's wishes] · get everyone's views in advance.
**Agenda:** the situation → the options → everyone's input → work to a decision → next steps.
**Ground rules:** one speaker at a time · focus on the parent's needs · no blame / no re-litigating history · shared goal.
**Include the person:** [involve them in deciding about their own life].
**Work through disagreement:** [surface the real tensions — load / money / differing views → toward a decision]; a mediator if stuck.
**End with:** [concrete next steps · who does what · when to revisit].

> Facilitates the decision — not legal, medical, or financial advice. Bring in the right professionals for those specifics.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Includes preparation so the meeting is grounded in shared facts
- [ ] Gets everyone's input, including quieter voices
- [ ] Sets ground rules against blame and old dynamics
- [ ] Includes the person being cared for in the decision
- [ ] Navigates the real disagreements toward a decision
- [ ] Ends with concrete owners and next steps; flags professionals for specifics

## Anti-Patterns
- **No preparation** — a feelings-fight with no shared facts.
- **Letting old family dynamics** or one loud voice run it.
- **Talking over the person** whose life it is.
- **Avoiding the real tensions** (money, unequal load).
- **A long emotional talk** with no decision or next steps.
