# Support the Bereaved

Most people freeze around grief and default to "let me know if you need anything" — which quietly puts the work on the griever and rarely gets used. This gives you better: words that actually land, specific help to offer instead of vague availability, a condolence in your own voice, and how to keep showing up after everyone else has moved on — so your care reaches them instead of just easing your own discomfort.

## What This Skill Produces

- **Words that land** — what to say (acknowledge, don't fix; name the person; "I'm so sorry" is enough) and the well-meant phrases to avoid ("everything happens for a reason," "at least…")
- **Concrete help, offered specifically** — instead of "let me know," a specific offer they can accept ("I'm bringing dinner Tuesday — okay?"; "I'll take the kids Saturday")
- **A condolence message** — a short, warm note in your voice, for a card, text, or in person
- **The long-haul plan** — showing up at weeks 4, 12, and on the anniversary, when the casseroles have stopped and the grief hasn't
- **A don't-center-yourself guide** — keeping the support about them, not your discomfort or your own losses
- **When to point to more** — gently, if they're struggling beyond what a friend can hold, the grief resources that help

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **Who's grieving and who they lost** — your relationship to them
- **How close you are** — which shapes what help is appropriate to offer
- **The moment** — just happened / the funeral / weeks or months on
- **What you can genuinely offer** — time, food, errands, presence — so offers are real

## Framework: Acknowledge, Offer Specifically, Stay

1. **Acknowledge, don't fix.** Grief isn't a problem to solve — "I'm so sorry, I'm here" beats any silver lining. Say the person's name; it's a gift, not a risk.
2. **Avoid the harmful comforts.** "At least…," "everything happens for a reason," and "they're in a better place" minimize — skip them.
3. **Offer something specific.** Replace "let me know if you need anything" with a concrete, easy-to-accept offer — you carry the initiative, not them.
4. **Show up for the long haul.** Support floods in week one and vanishes by week four — being there at week 12 and on the anniversary is where you matter most.
5. **Keep it about them.** Don't redirect to your own loss or need for reassurance; presence over performance.
6. **Know your limit.** If they're sinking beyond a friend's reach, gently point to grief counseling or a support group — and to a crisis line if there's any safety concern.

## Output Format

**Avoid:** ["at least…" · "everything happens for a reason" · "let me know if you need anything"].
**Offer specifically:** ["[concrete help] on [day] — okay?" — you carry the initiative].
**Condolence message:** "[short, warm, in your voice]."
**Long haul:** [check in at week 4 · week 12 · the anniversary].
**Keep it about them:** [presence over your own discomfort].
**If they're sinking:** [gently point to grief counseling/group; crisis line if safety's a concern].

- [ ] Keeps focus on the griever; points to more help when needed

## Anti-Patterns
- **"Let me know if you need anything"** — offloads the work onto them.
- **Silver-lining phrases** that minimize the loss.
- **Avoiding the person's name** or the subject entirely.
- **Vanishing after week one** when grief is long.
- **Centering your own feelings** or losses in their moment.
