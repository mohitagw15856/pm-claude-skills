# Deliberate-Practice Plan

Ten thousand hours of doing something the same way makes you consistent, not better. Improvement comes from *deliberate* practice: breaking a skill into pieces, drilling your weakest at the edge of your ability, and getting feedback to correct. This designs that — turning aimless repetition (which entrenches your current level) into focused practice that actually moves the needle.

## What This Skill Produces

- **The skill broken down** — into specific trainable sub-skills (you can't practice "be better at X" — you practice its components)
- **Weakness-targeted drills** — practice aimed at your specific weak sub-skills, at the edge of your current ability (not the fun, easy parts)
- **A feedback mechanism** — how you'll know if you did it right (a coach, a recording, a measurable target) — practice without feedback doesn't improve
- **A session structure** — a focused practice session (warm-up, targeted drill, feedback, adjust), kept short enough to stay effortful
- **Progress tracking** — how to measure that the drilling is working

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The skill** — what you want to get better at
- **Your weak spots** — where you're weakest (or a request to help identify them)
- **How you practice now** — to spot the mindless-repetition trap
- **Feedback available** — coach, recording, metrics, or none yet
- **Time** — how long/often you can practice

## Framework: Break Down, Drill The Weak, Get Feedback

1. **Decompose the skill.** Break it into specific sub-skills you can train separately — general practice improves nothing in particular.
2. **Target the weakness, not the strength.** Deliberate practice is uncomfortable because it drills what you're bad at, at the edge of your ability — not the parts you enjoy.
3. **Build in feedback.** Every rep needs a way to know if it was right — otherwise you practice mistakes. Set up the feedback source.
4. **Keep it effortful and focused.** Short, high-concentration sessions on the target sub-skill beat long autopilot ones. Full attention is the point.
5. **Adjust from feedback.** Correct after each rep/session — the loop is drill → feedback → adjust → drill.

## Output Format

### Deliberate practice: [skill]

**Sub-skills:** [broken into trainable components].
**Drill your weakness:** [the weak sub-skill] at [the edge of your ability] via [specific drill].
**Feedback:** [how you'll know if it's right — coach/recording/metric].
**Session structure:** [warm-up → targeted drill → feedback → adjust], ~[short duration].
**Track:** [how to measure the drill is working].

## Quality Checks
- [ ] The skill is broken into specific trainable sub-skills
- [ ] Practice targets the weakness at the edge of ability, not the fun parts
- [ ] A feedback mechanism is built in
- [ ] Sessions are short and effortful, not long and mindless
- [ ] There's a way to track that it's working

## Anti-Patterns
- **Mindless repetition** of the whole skill.
- **Practicing your strengths** because they're enjoyable.
- **No feedback loop** — practicing mistakes.
- **Long autopilot sessions** instead of short focused ones.
- **"Just do it more"** with no structure.

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "How do I practice basketball shooting effectively?"
- "My guitar practice isn't making me better — design a real routine."
- "Deliberate practice plan for improving my writing."
- "I want to get better at public speaking — how should I practice?"
- "Break down this skill so I can actually train the weak parts."
