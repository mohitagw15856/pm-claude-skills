# 融资商业计划书（BP）

Chinese investors read a great many BPs quickly, often on a phone, and decide from the first few pages whether to take a meeting. They look for a clear line on what the company does, proof that the team can do it, and numbers that hold up. This skill builds the BP around evidence the founders actually have and prepares them for the questions and clauses that follow.

Write in Simplified Chinese.

## What This Skill Produces

- A one-sentence positioning and a three-sentence summary
- A slide outline of twelve to fifteen pages with the content and evidence for each
- A metrics table suited to the business model
- A financing plan: amount, valuation logic, dilution, use of funds, milestones
- Likely investor questions with answer notes
- Term-sheet clauses to understand, and a due-diligence preparation list

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:

- What the company does, for whom, and the current stage
- Team: founders' backgrounds, full-time status, equity
- Traction: users, revenue, growth, retention, pilots, signed customers
- Market: who buys, how many of them, what they spend now
- Business model and unit economics
- Competitors and what is different
- Round: amount sought, previous rounds and investors, valuation expectation
- Use of funds and the milestones the money will reach
- Who the BP is for: angel individuals, venture funds, government-guided funds, strategic investors

## Framework

1. **One sentence.** "我们为 [客户] 提供 [产品]，解决 [问题]，已经 [最强的一个数据]." If it needs a second sentence, the positioning is not ready.
2. **Storyline.** Problem that is real and costly, solution and product, why now, market size, business model, traction, competition, team, plan and financing.
3. **Market size from the bottom up.** Number of target customers times what each would pay per year. Top-down figures from reports serve as a cross-check only. Name sources.
4. **Traction over vision.** Charts of real numbers with dates. If early, show pilots, letters of intent, waitlists and what was learned.
5. **Metrics by model.** SaaS: ARR, net revenue retention, payback period. Consumer: active users, retention, acquisition cost, lifetime value. E-commerce: GMV, repeat rate, gross margin. Hard technology: technical milestones, yield, customer validation, orders.
6. **Competition.** A comparison on the dimensions customers care about, and an honest statement of where others are stronger.
7. **Team.** What each founder has done that is relevant, and why this team for this problem.
8. **Financing.** Amount, the share offered, the implied post-money valuation, use of funds by category, and the milestones reached in the runway. A common early-round range is ten to twenty-five percent dilution; give the reasoning, not a rule.
9. **Clauses to understand.** 对赌 (valuation adjustment), 回购 (redemption), 优先清算, 反稀释, 一票否决, 领售与随售, founder personal liability. Flag any clause that puts personal repayment on the founders.
10. **Due diligence.** Corporate records, cap table, IP ownership, contracts, financial statements, tax and social insurance compliance, employment agreements.
11. **Honesty.** Investors verify. Inflated numbers end the process and can create liability.

## Quality Checks

- [ ] The positioning fits in one sentence with one hard number
- [ ] Market size is built bottom-up with sources
- [ ] Every traction claim has a date and a source
- [ ] The financing plan ties money to milestones
- [ ] Founder-liability clauses are flagged
- [ ] No figure is invented or rounded up

## Anti-Patterns

- **Do not open with a trillion-yuan market.**
- **Do not write "no competitors".**
- **Do not present projections as results.**
- **Do not hide a weak metric.** Explain it.
- **Do not accept personal buy-back liability** without legal advice.
