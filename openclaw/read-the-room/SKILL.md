# Read the Room

Some people instinctively sense the mood, the power dynamics, and what's really being said under the words — others miss it entirely and step on landmines. This helps you read a situation: from what you describe, it interprets the likely dynamics (the vibe, who's driving, the unspoken tension, what people actually want), tells you how to check your read against reality, and how to adjust — while cautioning that reading a room is inference, not certainty.

## What This Skill Produces

- **The likely mood** — the emotional temperature and vibe you might be missing (tense, checked-out, competitive, warm)
- **The power/influence map** — who actually holds sway (not always the loudest or most senior), and whose buy-in matters
- **The unspoken layer** — the tensions, subtext, and what people seem to actually want vs. what's being said
- **How to verify** — ways to check your read (a test comment, a private ask, watching reactions) rather than acting on a guess
- **How to adjust** — the change in your approach the read suggests (when to push, hold back, ally, or wait)
- **An over-reading caution** — a reminder that inference isn't certainty and to stay curious, not paranoid

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The situation** — the meeting/gathering/interaction, and who's involved
- **What you've observed** — reactions, tone, body language, what's been said (the raw signals)
- **Your position** — your role/stake in it
- **What you're trying to do** — the goal you're navigating toward
- **What's confusing you** — where your read feels off or uncertain

## Framework: Interpret, Verify, Adjust

1. **Read the emotional temperature.** From the described signals, infer the likely mood — the thing that's felt but not said, which drives how people will actually respond.
2. **Map real influence.** Identify who actually holds sway and whose reaction matters most — often not the person talking most or ranking highest.
3. **Surface the subtext.** Name the likely unspoken tensions and what people seem to actually want, beneath the stated words.
4. **Hold it as a hypothesis.** A read is a guess — offer ways to test it (a probing comment, a quiet check with someone, watching a reaction) before betting on it.
5. **Adjust the approach.** Translate the read into a concrete change — push now vs. wait, whose buy-in to get first, what to avoid saying.
6. **Don't over-read.** Caution against paranoia and elaborate mind-reading; stay curious and verify rather than assume the worst.

## Output Format

### Reading: [the situation] · your goal [x]

**Likely mood:** [the emotional temperature you might be missing].
**Who actually holds sway:** [real influence, not just rank/volume].
**The unspoken layer:** [tensions + what people seem to actually want].
**Check your read:** [a way to verify before acting — test/ask/observe].
**Adjust your approach:** [push / hold / ally / wait — the concrete change].
**Don't over-read:** it's a hypothesis, not certainty — stay curious.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Infers the likely mood from the described signals
- [ ] Maps real influence, not just rank/volume
- [ ] Surfaces the unspoken subtext and what people want
- [ ] Frames the read as a hypothesis with a way to verify
- [ ] Translates it into a concrete approach adjustment
- [ ] Cautions against over-reading/paranoia

## Anti-Patterns
- **Stating the read as certain fact** from limited signals.
- **Only reading the surface** (rank, what's said).
- **Encouraging paranoid over-interpretation.**
- **A read with no verify step** or no approach adjustment.
- **Ignoring who actually holds influence.**

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "Help me read this meeting — I can't tell the vibe."
- "What's really going on in this situation at work?"
- "How should I play this socially? Something feels off."
- "I can't tell if my boss is annoyed or just busy — read it for me."
- "What's the unspoken dynamic in this group?"
