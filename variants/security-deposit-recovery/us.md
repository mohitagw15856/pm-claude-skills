# security-deposit-recovery — variant: US (state-varies)

*Overlay on `skills/security-deposit-recovery/SKILL.md`. States only what differs in the United States. Deposit law is **state law** — every figure below varies by state and changes; verify the current statute for your state before relying on it. Not legal advice. Updated 2026-09.*

## What's different here

**1. The return deadline is a state statute, and it's often short.** Most states require the landlord to return the deposit and/or an itemized statement within a fixed window after move-out — commonly **14 to 60 days** (examples to verify: California ~21 days; New York ~14 days; Texas ~30 days; Illinois ~30–45 days; some states have no statutory deadline). *A blown deadline is frequently the tenant's strongest argument — cite the elapsed days.*

**2. Itemization is usually mandatory.** Most states require a written, itemized list of deductions; several require receipts or estimates for repairs above a threshold. A deduction with no itemization is often unenforceable — the base skill's "itemization request" rung has statutory teeth here.

**3. Bad-faith withholding can carry multiplied damages.** Many states let a tenant recover **2× or 3× the deposit** (sometimes plus fees/costs) when a landlord withholds in bad faith or misses the statutory process (examples to verify: California up to 2× as a penalty; Texas up to 3× plus $100 and fees). The demand letter should name this category explicitly — it changes the landlord's calculation.

**4. Move-in inspection rules vary.** Some states require the landlord to offer a move-in/move-out inspection or a move-in checklist; where required and skipped, the landlord's later damage claims are weaker. Check whether your state requires one.

**5. The forum is small-claims court**, designed for these amounts, no lawyer expected. Limits vary widely by state (roughly **$5,000–$25,000**); filing fees are modest; some states require a demand letter first. Many states also have **state/local tenant-rights hotlines and legal-aid clinics** — use them before filing.

**6. Where the money must be kept.** Some states require deposits in a separate/escrow account or to pay interest — a landlord who commingled may have violated the statute. Worth one line in the letter where it applies.

## Bodies to name
- Your **state attorney general's consumer-protection office** (many take deposit complaints).
- **Local tenant unions / tenant-rights organisations** and **legal aid**.
- The county **small-claims court** self-help center.
