# security-deposit-recovery — variant: UK (England & Wales)

*Overlay on `skills/security-deposit-recovery/SKILL.md`. States only what differs in England and Wales (Scotland and Northern Ireland run separate schemes and rules — use `uk-scot` / `uk-ni` when those exist). Scheme rules and statute change; verify the current rules before relying on this. Not legal advice. Updated 2026-09.*

## What's different here — the whole game changes

**1. Deposits must be protected in a government-approved scheme.** For an assured shorthold tenancy, the landlord must place the deposit in one of the three approved schemes — **DPS (Deposit Protection Service), TDS (Tenancy Deposit Scheme), or MyDeposits** — **within 30 days** of receiving it, and give the tenant the **"prescribed information"** (which scheme, how to get it back, how to dispute). *Your first move is to check which scheme holds your deposit — each has an online lookup.*

**2. If it wasn't protected, the tenant has a strong claim.** A landlord who failed to protect the deposit or serve the prescribed information within 30 days can be ordered by a court to pay the tenant **between 1× and 3× the deposit** as a penalty (on top of returning it), and generally cannot use a Section 21 no-fault eviction until it's fixed. This often reverses the negotiation entirely — check protection status *before* arguing about deductions.

**3. Disputes go to the scheme's free ADR, not to court first.** If the landlord proposes deductions you reject, you raise a dispute through the scheme's **free alternative dispute resolution** service. Crucially, **the burden is on the landlord** to evidence each deduction (check-in/check-out inventories, photos, receipts) — a landlord without a check-in inventory usually loses. The base skill's evidence protocol maps directly onto what the adjudicator wants.

**4. The deposit is capped.** Under the Tenant Fees Act 2019, a deposit is capped at **5 weeks' rent** (annual rent under £50,000) or 6 weeks above that. A deposit over the cap was itself unlawful to charge.

**5. Timing.** The landlord should return the undisputed amount within **10 days** of you both agreeing what's due; the scheme sets the dispute timetable. There's no US-style fixed statutory return deadline — the scheme process *is* the deadline.

**6. Fair wear and tear is the standard the adjudicators apply**, with **betterment** rules: a landlord can't charge full replacement for an aged item — apportionment by the item's age/lifespan is expected (the base skill's depreciation math is exactly what adjudicators do).

## Bodies to name
- The **scheme** holding your deposit (DPS / TDS / MyDeposits) — its ADR service.
- **Shelter** and **Citizens Advice** (free housing advice).
- The **county court** (small-claims track) only if ADR isn't available or for the non-protection penalty claim.

## Letter language to add
> "Please confirm which approved scheme holds my deposit and provide the prescribed information. If the deposit was not protected within 30 days of receipt, I reserve the right to seek the statutory penalty of up to three times the deposit. Otherwise I dispute the proposed deductions and will refer the matter to the scheme's dispute resolution service, where the burden of evidencing each deduction rests with the landlord."
