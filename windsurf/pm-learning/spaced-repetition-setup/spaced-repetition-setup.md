# Spaced-Repetition Setup

You forget most of what you learn within days — unless you review it at expanding intervals, right as you're about to forget. Spaced repetition exploits that. But most people make terrible cards (whole paragraphs, ambiguous prompts) and quit. This sets up a system that works: how to write cards that stick, the review rhythm, what's even worth memorizing, and the traps that kill the habit.

## What This Skill Produces

- **Card-writing rules** — atomic (one fact per card), testable, unambiguous, and in your own words — the difference between cards that work and cards you'll delete
- **A review cadence** — how spacing works (review just before you'd forget; intervals expand as you succeed) and how to sustain it
- **What to make cards for** — the facts/concepts worth memorizing vs. what's better learned by doing (don't card everything)
- **The failure modes** — the specific mistakes (giant cards, ambiguous prompts, memorizing without understanding, letting reviews pile up) that make people quit
- **A starter setup** — tool suggestion and a first batch of well-formed cards for your topic

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **What you're memorizing** — a subject, a language, facts for an exam
- **Why** — an exam, a skill, long-term retention
- **Your tool** — Anki, a notes app, paper, or need a suggestion
- **How much material** — and any deadline

## Framework: Good Cards, Right Rhythm, Sustained

1. **Make cards atomic.** One fact or idea per card, phrased as a clear question with an unambiguous answer — big cards and vague prompts are why systems fail.
2. **Understand before memorizing.** Cards cement understanding; they don't create it. Learn the concept first, then card the facts (or it's meaningless rote).
3. **Trust the spacing.** Review items just before you'd forget; intervals lengthen as you get them right. The system schedules it — your job is to show up.
4. **Card selectively.** Memorize what genuinely needs recall (vocabulary, facts, formulas); learn procedural stuff by doing, not by flashcard.
5. **Sustain the habit.** Small daily reviews beat weekend binges; don't let cards pile up (the #1 quit reason). Keep the daily load small.

## Output Format

### Spaced repetition: [subject] · tool [x]

**Write cards like this:** atomic (one fact) · clear question · unambiguous answer · your own words. Example: [a good card vs a bad one for your topic].
**Review rhythm:** [daily short reviews; intervals expand as you succeed] — don't let them pile up.
**Make cards for:** [facts/vocab/formulas]. **Don't card:** [procedural stuff — learn by doing].
**Avoid:** giant cards · vague prompts · memorizing without understanding · binge-and-lapse.
**Starter batch:** [a few well-formed cards for your topic].

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Card-writing rules emphasize atomic, testable, unambiguous cards
- [ ] Stresses understanding before memorizing
- [ ] Explains the expanding-interval review rhythm
- [ ] Says what to card vs. learn by doing
- [ ] Names the failure modes that cause quitting
- [ ] Provides a starter setup and example cards

## Anti-Patterns
- **Wall-of-text cards** with paragraphs to memorize.
- **Ambiguous prompts** with multiple right answers.
- **Memorizing without understanding** first.
- **Carding everything**, including procedural skills.
- **Letting reviews pile up** into an unmanageable backlog.
