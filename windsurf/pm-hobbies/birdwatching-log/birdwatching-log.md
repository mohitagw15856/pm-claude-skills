# Birdwatching Log

Birding is one of the cheapest, most rewarding hobbies — the birds are already outside. This helps a beginner know what to expect locally, learn the field marks that separate the tricky pairs, and keep a simple, satisfying log of what they've seen, without needing expensive gear or a lifetime of knowledge.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A likely-species list** — common birds for your region and season, with what makes each recognizable
- **ID prompts** — the specific field marks (size, bill, color patches, behavior) and calls that separate look-alikes
- **Gear & timing** — realistic beginner kit (binoculars help, phone works) and the best times/places to look
- **A life-list format** — a simple, motivating way to log species, date, and place
- **A confirm step** — how to verify a specific bird with a live photo/sound ID app

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **Location & habitat** — region, and backyard/park/coast/woodland
- **Season** — time of year (drives migrants vs residents)
- **What you saw** — for an ID: size, colors, bill shape, behavior, sound, where
- **Gear** — naked eye, binoculars, camera, or just a phone
- **Goal** — casual backyard watching or building a life-list

## Framework: Notice, Narrow, Log

1. **Set expectations by place and season.** Suggest what's actually around — backyard feeders, park waterfowl, coastal waders — not exotic rarities.
2. **Teach the field marks that matter.** Size, bill shape, wing bars, eye rings, tail, and behavior narrow an ID faster than color alone.
3. **Use sound.** Many species are heard before seen; describe the call in words and point to a sound-ID app to confirm.
4. **Narrow the look-alikes.** For confusing pairs, name the one or two features that reliably separate them.
5. **Log to keep momentum.** A tidy life-list (species, date, place, notes) turns casual sightings into a growing collection you want to add to.

## Output Format

### Birding: [location] · [habitat] · [season] · [gear]

**Likely to see:** [species — the giveaway mark] ×5–8.

**For an ID** ([your description]): most likely [species] — confirm by [field mark / call]. Easily confused with [look-alike]; tell them apart by [feature].

**Gear & timing:** [kit] · best at [dawn/dusk], at [place].
**Life-list entry:** | Species | Date | Place | Notes |

> For a specific bird, confirm with a live photo/sound ID app — plumage varies by age, sex, and season.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Species suggested fit the location, habitat, and season
- [ ] ID guidance uses field marks/behavior/sound, not just color
- [ ] Confusing look-alikes are separated by a specific feature
- [ ] Gear advice is beginner-realistic (phone is fine)
- [ ] Provides a simple life-list format and points to a live ID app

## Anti-Patterns
- **Suggesting rarities** a beginner won't see instead of common locals.
- **Color-only IDs** that ignore size, bill, and behavior.
- **Asserting a confident species ID** from a vague description — offer the likely options and a confirm step.
- **Gatekeeping gear** — implying you need expensive binoculars to start.
- **Ignoring season/migration** when suggesting species.

## Example Trigger Phrases
- "I want to start birdwatching in my backyard — what will I see?"
- "Small brown bird with a red head at my feeder — what is it?"
- "How do I tell a crow from a raven?"
- "Set me up a birding life-list."
- "Best time and gear to start birding near the coast?"
