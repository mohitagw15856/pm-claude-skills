# Reconnect With Someone

Most lost friendships didn't end — they just drifted, and then the gap itself became the barrier ("it's been so long, it'd be weird now"). But the other person almost always feels the same and is glad you reached out. This gets you past the awkwardness with a warm message that acknowledges the gap lightly, gives a genuine reason for reaching out, and reopens the door — because reconnecting usually just needs one person brave enough to text first.

## What This Skill Produces

- **The awkwardness reframe** — why the gap feels bigger to you than to them, and why they'll likely be glad, not annoyed
- **A warm reach-out message** — one that acknowledges the time lightly (not a groveling apology) and feels genuine
- **A specific hook** — the reason for reaching out now (a shared memory, something that reminded you of them, a life update, or simply "you crossed my mind")
- **The path forward** — how to move from the reopening message toward actually reconnecting (a call, a meet-up) without overloading the first message
- **A no-pressure stance** — how to reach out warmly without making them feel guilty for the drift either

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **Who** — the person and your history (close friend, old colleague, relative)
- **The gap** — how long, and roughly why you drifted (moved, life, a small falling-out)
- **What prompted it** — why you want to reconnect now
- **Any complication** — was there tension, or did it just fade

## Framework: Acknowledge Lightly, Give A Hook, Reopen

1. **Shrink the awkwardness.** Remind the person that the other side almost certainly feels the gap too and will be glad — the barrier is mostly in their own head.
2. **Acknowledge the time, lightly.** A brief, warm nod to the gap ("I know it's been ages!") beats either ignoring it or a heavy apology that makes it awkward.
3. **Give a genuine hook.** A specific reason lands better than a bare "hey" — a memory, something that reminded you of them, a life update, or an honest "you popped into my head and I miss you."
4. **Keep the first message light.** Reopen the door — don't cram a friendship's worth of catching-up or a big ask into the first text.
5. **Move toward real reconnection.** Once they reply, suggest a low-key call or meet-up — the message is the door, the conversation is the room.
6. **No guilt, either way.** Reach out warmly without blaming them (or yourself) for the drift — keep it light and forward-looking.

## Output Format

### Reconnecting with: [person] · gap [time] · [why you drifted]

**The reframe:** [why they'll likely be glad, not annoyed — the awkwardness is mostly yours].
**Your message**
> [Warm, light acknowledgment of the gap + a specific genuine hook + an easy opening — not a heavy apology or a wall of text].

**The hook you're using:** [memory / reminder / update / "you crossed my mind"].
**Then:** once they reply, [suggest a low-key call/meet-up].
**Keep it:** no guilt, warm, forward-looking.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Reframes the awkwardness as smaller than it feels
- [ ] Acknowledges the gap lightly, without over-apologizing
- [ ] Includes a specific, genuine hook for reaching out
- [ ] Keeps the first message light (a door, not the whole room)
- [ ] Shows how to move toward actually reconnecting
- [ ] Avoids guilt-tripping them (or self)

## Anti-Patterns
- **A heavy apology** for the gap that makes it awkward.
- **A bare "hey"** with no hook or warmth.
- **Cramming everything** into the first message.
- **Guilt-tripping** them for the drift.
- **Never sending it** because it feels too awkward (the whole barrier).
