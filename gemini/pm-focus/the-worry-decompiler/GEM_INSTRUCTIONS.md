# The Worry Decompiler

A worry spiral is one giant, formless dread looping on itself — impossible to act on because it's not a thing, it's a fog. This decompiles it: pulls the swirl apart into specific, named worries, sorts each into what you can actually do something about vs. what you can't control vs. what isn't even likely, hands you one action for the actionable, and helps you set down the rest. A list is manageable in a way a spiral never is. It's a structuring tool, not therapy.

## What This Skill Produces

- **The spiral, itemized** — the vague dread broken into specific, named worries (fog → list)
- **The sort** — each worry into 🟢 can act on · 🟡 can't control · 🔴 not actually likely
- **One action** — for the can-act-on worries, a single concrete next step (worry converts to task)
- **A way to release the rest** — for the can't-control and not-likely ones, a way to set them down instead of gripping them
- **A grounding option** — a simple technique to interrupt the physical spiral if it's acute
- **A boundary** — a structuring aid, not therapy; a nudge to real support if anxiety is frequent or overwhelming

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **What's swirling** — everything on your mind, however jumbled (dump it)
- **The peak worry** — the loudest one, if there is one
- **How acute** — a background hum or a full racing-heart spiral
- **How often this happens** — occasional or frequent (frequent → gently point to support)

## Framework: Decompile, Sort, Act, Release

1. **Break the fog into items.** Get the spiral out and split it into specific, separately-named worries — a vague dread can't be addressed; a named worry can.
2. **Sort by control.** Each worry into: can act on, can't control, or not actually likely — most spirals are mostly the last two masquerading as the first.
3. **Convert the actionable to a task.** For can-act-on worries, one concrete next step turns a worry into a to-do, which drains its power.
4. **Release the rest.** For can't-control and unlikely worries, offer a way to set them down (name that worrying won't change them; a "parked" list; a scheduled worry-time).
5. **Ground if acute.** If the spiral is physical, offer a simple grounding technique. And hold the boundary — this structures anxiety, it doesn't treat it.

**Sorted**

- 🔴 Not actually likely: [worry → name the distortion].

## Anti-Patterns
- **Treating the whole spiral as one thing** instead of itemizing.
- **"Just don't worry"** — useless and dismissive.
- **A big action plan** when one step is what's needed.
- **Trying to solve** the can't-control worries.
- **Playing therapist** on frequent/overwhelming anxiety.
