# Burnout Recovery Plan

Burnout isn't cured by a weekend off — it comes back if the conditions that caused it don't change. Recovery is two-track: immediate relief to stop the freefall, and structural changes to the drivers (unsustainable load, no control, no reward, unfairness, values mismatch). This helps you diagnose your drivers and build a plan you can actually act on — while being clear that severe cases need real support.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A driver diagnosis** — which burnout factors are hitting you (workload, lack of control, insufficient reward, unfairness, community breakdown, values conflict)
- **Immediate relief** — the first steps to reduce acute strain and create breathing room
- **Root-cause changes** — the boundary, workload, and role changes (and the conversations with your manager) that address *why* it happened
- **A recovery timeline** — honest expectations that recovery takes time and isn't linear
- **When it's bigger** — a clear flag that severe, persistent burnout — or signs of depression/anxiety — needs a professional
- **A sustainability check** — how to prevent the slide back

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The signs** — exhaustion, cynicism, dread, reduced performance, physical symptoms
- **The context** — job/role, workload, control, and what specifically drains you
- **How long** — recent or long-running (affects severity)
- **What you can change** — leverage at work, financial constraints, support available
- **Severity** — coping but depleted, or genuinely unable to function

## Framework: Relieve, Then Fix The Cause

1. **Diagnose the drivers.** Burnout has known contributors (load, control, reward, fairness, community, values) — identify which are active, because the fix depends on the cause.
2. **Create immediate relief.** Reduce acute strain now — use leave if possible, drop or defer non-essentials, and restore basic rest, movement, and connection.
3. **Change the root, not just rest.** Rest without changing the conditions relapses — plan the boundaries, workload renegotiation, or role/values shifts, including a concrete manager conversation.
4. **Set realistic timelines.** Recovery is gradual and non-linear; frame expectations so a bad day isn't read as failure.
5. **Know when it's beyond self-help.** Persistent, severe burnout or signs of depression/anxiety warrant a doctor or therapist — say this plainly.
6. **Build sustainability.** Put in the ongoing habits and boundaries that stop the slide back.

> Not medical advice. If burnout is severe or persistent, or you notice signs of depression or anxiety, please talk to a doctor or therapist.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Diagnoses the specific burnout drivers, not just "you're tired"
- [ ] Provides immediate relief steps
- [ ] Addresses root causes (boundaries/workload/role), not only rest
- [ ] Includes a concrete manager conversation where relevant
- [ ] Sets realistic, non-linear recovery expectations
- [ ] Flags when to seek professional help

## Anti-Patterns
- **"Take a vacation"** as the whole answer — relapse guaranteed.
- **Only symptom relief** with no change to the causes.
- **Ignoring the workplace drivers** the person can influence.
- **Promising a fast fix.**
- **Missing signs** that need medical/therapeutic help.
