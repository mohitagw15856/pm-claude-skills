# Client Offboarding

How a project ends is what the client remembers — and most freelancers just... stop, leaving referrals, testimonials, and repeat work on the table. A deliberate offboarding delivers a clean handoff, a strong final impression, and asks (at the moment of peak goodwill) that turn one finished project into the next three. This builds that closeout, whether it's a one-off wrap or ending an ongoing relationship.

## What This Skill Produces

- **A closeout checklist** — final deliverables, transferring access/files, the final invoice, and any documentation the client needs to run without you
- **A wrap-up/handoff message** — a warm, professional close that summarizes what was delivered and the results
- **The peak-moment asks** — the testimonial, referral, and repeat-work/retainer asks, made when goodwill is highest
- **Ongoing-relationship offboarding** — how to end a retainer or long engagement gracefully (notice, transition, no burned bridge)
- **Keep-the-door-open** — how to stay in light contact so future work flows back to you
- **Loose-end prevention** — the handoff details that stop post-project "quick questions" dragging on unpaid

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if not provided:
- **The engagement** — what you delivered, one-off or ongoing, and how it's ending
- **The relationship** — how it went, and how warm the client is
- **What's outstanding** — final deliverables, payment, access to transfer
- **Your goals** — referrals, a testimonial, repeat work, or a clean exit
- **Any tension** — if it's ending on a sour note (changes the approach)

## Framework: Close Clean, Ask At The Peak, Leave The Door Open

1. **Close it out cleanly.** Deliver everything, transfer access and files, send the final invoice, and provide any documentation so the client isn't stranded — professionalism they'll remember.
2. **End on a strong note.** A warm wrap-up that recaps what was achieved (and the results) reinforces the value and the good feeling.
3. **Ask at peak goodwill.** The end of a successful project is the best moment to request a testimonial, ask for referrals, and propose repeat work or a retainer — make these while enthusiasm is high.
4. **Offboard ongoing work gracefully.** For a retainer/long engagement, give proper notice, plan the transition/handover, and part on good terms even if you're the one ending it.
5. **Leave the door open.** A light stay-in-touch plan (a check-in, occasional value) keeps you top-of-mind for future needs and referrals.
6. **Prevent unpaid drag.** Set expectations on post-project support so "one more quick thing" doesn't become unpaid ongoing work.

## Anti-Patterns
- **Just stopping** with no closeout or final impression.
- **Leaving the client stranded** without access/docs.
- **Missing the peak moment** to ask for testimonial/referral/repeat.
- **Burning a bridge** when ending an ongoing engagement.
- **No boundaries** on post-project "quick questions."
